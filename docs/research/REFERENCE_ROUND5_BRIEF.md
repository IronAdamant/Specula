# Reference, round five: the trunk, the shoulder, hands and feet

Status: **asked for** (2026-11-19, at the owner's word: "trunk and shoulder muscle and surface anatomy
from front, side and back, plus hands and feet from several angles -- Get me full list of that please").
Nothing in hand waits on it.

**Why these.** The figure ([FIGURE.md](../design/FIGURE.md)) now measures within a few per cent of its
body, and the form views ([FORM.md](../design/FORM.md)) show what is still wrong with its shape: a flat
plate on the chest and on the back, an arm that lies on the shoulder as a separate capsule, a trunk
like a box, a seam where the legs meet it, and hands and feet that are right in size and plain in
form. Those are faults of surface anatomy, and they are mended by looking at how the real surface
runs. The pictures are looked at and compared with captures, never traced.

**In hand already, so not wanted again:** Richer's skeleton of the trunk (front, back, side) and his
whole-figure plates; Albinus's plates of the head, neck and trunk and of the shoulder girdle with the
ribs; Stratz's surface muscles of a female torso (front and back); Richer's hips and pelvis; and the
hands and feet of rounds one and two (mostly bones, a few surface views).
The lines below ask for what those do not show.

## The rules, as before

- **Pictures** come with an index row each: file, what it shows, view, subject (sex, build, age if
  stated), source page, author, licence with its link, date checked. They go into the pictures'
  repository, [specula-imagines](https://github.com/IronAdamant/specula-imagines).
- **Numbers** come as library files in the house form: every value with its source, its page or table
  and a link; who was measured; "Not found" and "Sources disagree" at the end; "computed by me" for
  arithmetic on cited numbers.
- **Licences:** public domain and CC0 first, then CC BY and CC BY-SA with the author named. Anything
  else is a link and not a file. A licence is read from its source, never guessed.
- **People in pictures:** unclothed adults are kept apart (`ADULTS-ONLY/`). **No unclothed figure of
  anyone under 18, drawn or photographed, and none whose subject cannot be shown to be an adult**:
  leave those out, links included. A plate by Muybridge is held against his own catalogue of models
  (*Prospectus and Catalogue of Plates*, 1887) before it is sent: the model's number and what the
  catalogue says of that model go in the index row. Hands and feet alone, and anatomical drawings of
  muscle and bone, are not unclothed figures.
- **Size:** 2,000 pixels or more on the long side where the source has it. A plate with its labels
  and its key, not a crop without them.

## 1. The trunk, as a surface

For each line: **front, back, side and three-quarter**, of a man and of a woman, standing at rest.
Two builds of each where they can be found (lean and muscular; average or soft), because the figure
is one recipe for every build.

| Wanted | What it must show |
|---|---|
| The chest, front | the pectoral's lower and outer border; how it thins toward the breastbone and thickens toward the armpit; the hollow at the breastbone; the collarbones and the pit between them; on a woman, how the breast sits on the pectoral and where its lower fold lies |
| The chest and the flank, side | the depth of the rib cage against the depth of the belly; the serratus's fingers over the ribs; the line from the armpit to the hip |
| The belly, front and side | the rectus and its bands; the line down the middle; the navel's height; the oblique's bulge over the hip bone; the fold of the groin where the trunk meets the thigh |
| The back, whole | the furrow of the spine and the two columns beside it; the shoulder blades' outline at rest; the trapezius's diamond; the latissimus's sweep to the arm; the dimples over the pelvis; the triangle over the sacrum |
| The neck into the trunk, all four views | the sternomastoid; the trapezius's slope to the shoulder; the seventh neck vertebra; the pit above the collarbone |
| The rib cage's shape, without its muscles | from the front, the side, above and below: an egg, not a box; how much wider it is than deep at the level of the nipples and of the tenth rib |
| Cross-sections of the trunk | at the shoulders, the nipples, the waist, the navel and the hips, with their outlines: the Visible Human's sections (public domain) or an atlas's. **Most wanted of this section**: the figure's trunk is built from such sections |
| The buttocks and the hip, back and side | the gluteal fold; the hollow at the side of the hip behind the great trochanter; how the buttock meets the thigh and the back |

## 2. The shoulder, and the arm where it joins the trunk

The figure's worst fault. Each line **front, back, side and from above**.

| Wanted | What it must show |
|---|---|
| The deltoid, at rest | its three parts; how it wraps the head of the arm bone and comes to a point halfway down the upper arm; its border with the pectoral (the groove) and with the trapezius |
| The shoulder from above | the collarbone, the point of the shoulder (the acromion), the shoulder blade's spine: the shoulder's outline seen from over the head. Rare; ask for it by name |
| The armpit | arm hanging, arm level, arm raised: the front fold (pectoral) and the back fold (latissimus and teres), and the hollow between |
| The arm raised, in steps | at 0, 45, 90, 135 and 180 degrees to the side, and the same forward: from the front and from the back. How the shoulder blade turns and the collarbone lifts, and how the outline from neck to arm changes |
| The shoulder blade moving | drawn forward, pulled back, shrugged, pressed down: from the back |
| The shoulder girdle as bone | collarbone, shoulder blade and arm bone together, from the front, the back, above and the side, with the joint's centre marked if a source marks it |
| The upper arm into the elbow | biceps, triceps and brachialis from the front, back and both sides; the elbow straight and bent to 90 and 140 degrees, with the point of the elbow and the two knobs beside it |
| The forearm turned | palm up, palm down and between, from the front and the back: how the two bones cross and the outline changes |

## 3. Hands

Each view of **a man's and a woman's hand**, at rest unless said. Photographs and drawings both:
photographs for the surface, drawings for what lies under it.

| Wanted | What it must show |
|---|---|
| Six views of the open hand | the palm, the back, the thumb's side, the little finger's side, from the fingertips, from the wrist |
| The palm's pads and creases | the thumb's mound, the mound under the little finger, the pads at the roots of the fingers, the hollow between; the three main creases |
| The back of the hand | the knuckles' arc, the tendons to each finger, the web between the fingers and how far down it reaches, the web of the thumb |
| One finger, three views | side, back and underneath, straight and bent at each joint: the pads, the creases, the knuckle's shape, the nail and its bed |
| The thumb | its three bones and its saddle joint; across the palm, out to the side, opposed to each fingertip |
| The hand in poses | relaxed (its natural curl), a fist from four sides, a flat push, pointing, a pinch, a grip round a rod of 3 cm and of 6 cm, a grip on a ball, a grip on a sword's hilt and on a handle, spread wide |
| The wrist | bent up, down and to each side, with how far; the two knobs of the forearm's bones; the tendons that stand out on the inside |
| Bones and muscles of the hand | a labelled plate from the palm and from the back, with the joints' axes if marked |
| Builds and ages | a broad working hand, a slender hand, an old hand: adults only |

## 4. Feet

Each view of **a man's and a woman's foot**, standing with weight on it unless said.

| Wanted | What it must show |
|---|---|
| Six views of the foot | inside (the arch's side), outside, from the front, from behind (the heel and the heel cord), from above, the sole |
| The sole and its print | the heel's pad, the ball, the toes' pads, the arch off the ground; a footprint of a low, a middle and a high arch |
| The arches | the long arch on the inside, the low one on the outside, the one across: as bone and as surface |
| The ankle | the inner knob higher and further forward than the outer; the heel cord and the hollows beside it; from the front, both sides and behind |
| The toes | from above and from the side: the great toe's line, how the others curl and step down in length; the nails |
| The foot moving | on tiptoe (from the side and behind), toes lifted, foot pointed, foot pulled up, rolled in and out, with how far each goes |
| The foot through a step | heel strike, flat, heel off, toe off: from the side, with where the weight is. Plates or frames of a recorded walk |
| Unloaded against loaded | the same foot hanging and standing: how much it spreads and lengthens |
| Bones and muscles of the foot and lower leg | labelled plates from the inside, the outside, above and below; the calf's two heads and the heel cord from behind and the side |
| In a shoe and a boot | how a shoe changes the foot's outline, for figures that are dressed: side and front |

## 5. Numbers that go with them (library files)

| Wanted | |
|---|---|
| Hand dimensions | lengths and breadths of each finger's three segments and of the palm, the thumb's, knuckle breadths and depths, the hand's thickness, by sex, with the spread. The US Army's hand survey (Greiner, 1991, public domain) is the first place to look; say how it joins ANSUR II's hand length and breadth |
| Foot dimensions | length, breadth at the ball and at the heel, the ball's place along the foot, instep height, ankle height, the toes' lengths, arch height by type, by sex. ANSUR II has some; the Army's foot surveys have more |
| Joint ranges | every joint of the hand and wrist, the ankle and the toes; the shoulder in all its directions, and how much of raising the arm is the shoulder blade's (the ratio by angle); the collarbone's lift and turn |
| Where the joints' centres are | the shoulder's centre against the acromion; the finger joints' centres against the knuckles; the ankle's axis against the two knobs: as offsets or shares of a segment |
| Trunk sections as numbers | breadth and depth of the rib cage and the trunk at each level in section 1, and how round or square each outline is (a superellipse's exponent, or the ratio of the section's area to its box's), by sex and build |
| Muscle thickness | how thick the pectoral, the deltoid, the latissimus, the trapezius, the gluteus, the rectus and the calf are over the bone, by sex and by lean, average and muscular (ultrasound or MRI studies) |
| Fat under the skin | skinfold thickness by site (chest, belly, hip, back under the shoulder blade, back of the arm, thigh, calf), by sex and build: it is what rounds the surface over the muscle |
| Where muscles attach | origin and insertion of the large surface muscles of the trunk and shoulder as shares along their bones, where an atlas gives them |

## 6. Where to look first

Not a limit: where the earlier rounds found the most.

- **Public domain atlases and artists' anatomies:** Richer, *Anatomie artistique* (1890) and
  *Nouvelle anatomie artistique*; Gray's *Anatomy* (1918 edition); Albinus; Vesalius; Marshall,
  *Anatomy for Artists* (1878); Rimmer, *Art Anatomy* (1877); Vanderpoel, *The Human Figure* (1907);
  Thomson, *A Handbook of Anatomy for Art Students*; Duval; Braus, *Anatomie des Menschen*, where its
  date allows.
- **The Visible Human Project** (US National Library of Medicine, public domain): cross-sections.
- **Wikimedia Commons** photographs and the *Anatomography* and *BodyParts3D* renders (CC BY-SA:
  name the author and the licence).
- **US government surveys and reports** (public domain): the Army's hand and foot surveys, NASA's
  *Anthropometric Source Book*, the *Man-Systems Integration Standards* for joint ranges.
- **Open-access papers** (CC BY) for muscle thickness, skinfolds, shoulder rhythm and joint centres.

## 7. What to say when something is not found

As before: a line in the delivery's coverage map ("not found", "found only under a licence that does
not allow a copy: link", "found for men only"). A gap that is said is worth more than a picture that
is nearly right.
