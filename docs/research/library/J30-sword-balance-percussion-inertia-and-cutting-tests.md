## 1. Physics used (from the opened sources)

- Pulaczewski 2018 (Appendix 1, repository PDF p. 20): moment of inertia about the pivot I = R^2 M, with R^2 = L d, so I = L d M. L = distance from the pivot to the centre of percussion (CoP); d = pivot to centre of mass (found by balancing on a knife edge); M = mass. Rotational kinetic energy of a cut = 1/2 I omega^2.
- "Quantifying the Properties of Your Sword", Uni Kiel online text (author not shown in the snippet) (https://www.tf.uni-kiel.de/matwis/amat/iss/kap_c/advanced/ac_3_2.html), read via search snippet only (SECONDARY / snippet): pendulum method I_A = T^2 m g p / (4 pi^2); CoP from the pivot s = I_PP / (m p). Second check (2026-11-18): the page now opens (HTTP 200); both formulas are on it (I_A = T²·m·g·p/4π², and s_C = I_PP/(m·p_PC)), and the author is shown at the bottom as "© H. Föll (Iron, Steel and Swords script)". So this row can be treated as opened, not snippet only.
- Ensis Sub Caelo, post by "Vincent" (surname not shown on the page), "Measuring swords as pendulums", 2017 (https://blog.subcaelo.net/ensis/measuring-swords-pendulums/; the Kiel page credits a related figure to "Vincent Le Chevalier's homepage", which suggests a surname, but the blog does not confirm it, second check 2026-11-18): hilt-down pendulum rig; he times 20 periods by default. Check object: a 1 m x 3 cm x 2 mm steel profile measured at radius of gyration 28.65 cm +/- 2 mm against a theoretical 28.87 cm. He reports that the waggle test "can be both biased and unreliable" (citing a study by Robert Geissler) and that pendulum tests are more reliable.

## 2. Measured antique swords: Pulaczewski 2018

Source: Pulaczewski, Maciej (2018) "The Colichemarde: Historical Curiosity or Efficient Weapon?" The Journal of the Arms and Armour Society XXII (6), pp. 356-384. Repository copy: https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/34663/ . Licence (read from the cover sheet): copyright retained by the author ("Paper © Maciej Pulaczewski 2018"); free for personal research or study with credit, unchanged. (Second check, 2026-11-18: the cover sheet, PDF p. 1, carries the repository's personal-research terms; the "Paper © Maciej Pulaczewski 2018" line is on PDF p. 2, the journal notice.) NOT an open licence; facts retyped only. Page numbers below are PDF page numbers of the repository copy (the PDF has a cover sheet).

Masses (Table 2, PDF p. 7):
- 13 colichemardes in the Royal Armouries, Leeds (hilt included): mean 466 g, SD 78.7 g, range 320 to 640 g. Without the outlier IX 2609 (640 g): mean 451 g, SD 61 g. Table 2 lists IX 2804 twice (as "IX 2804" and "IX2804", both 453 g), so the n = 13 mean may count one sword twice (second check, 2026-11-18; see Sources disagree).
- 13 ordinary small-swords, 1680s to 1820s, weighed by the author: mean 352 g (no SD given).

Centre of percussion as a fraction of blade length, measured from the hilt (Appendix 2, PDF pp. 23-24):
- 22 small-swords in private collections (Table Appendix 2.1): mean 0.34, SD 0.06, range 0.17 (1830s English cut-steel court sword) to 0.44 (1787 colichemarde sword-stick).
- 11 colichemardes, Royal Armouries (Table Appendix 2.2): mean 0.24, SD 0.03, range 0.21 to 0.32.
- Text (PDF p. 17): a CoP beyond about 0.4 of the blade "would make the sword feel 'blade-heavy'".

Sabre vs colichemarde (Table Appendix 1.1, PDF p. 22; pivot at the hand):

Kinetic energy (Table Appendix 1.2, PDF p. 22; arm mass taken as 2.84 to 3.48 kg, fencer 56.8 to 69.6 kg): colichemarde thrust 2.39 to 3.16 J; colichemarde lunge 13.0 to 14.7 J; sabre cut 3.53 to 4.37 J; sabre lunge 14.9 to 16.8 J.

Critical buckling force, blade fixed at the hand and pushed tip-down onto a digital balance, +/- 1 N (Table 3, PDF p. 18): colichemarde 150 N; russet-handled small-sword (hollow triangular) 103 N; hollow diamond 82.3 N; rhomboidal 72.5 N; silver-handled (hollow triangular) 69.6 N; French military (hollow triangular) 68.6 N; mourning sword (lentoid) 59.8 N; modern fencing foil (rectangular) 50.0 N.

## 4. Cutting and thrusting tests on textile armour and mail (SECONDARY, informal)

Source: Michael Edelson (New York Historical Fencing Association), "Riveted Maille and Padded Jack Tests", myArmoury forum, posted 12 Sep 2007. https://myarmoury.com/talk/viewtopic.php?t=11131 . Informal single-tester trials; no force, speed or energy was measured for the sword blows. Licence: forum post, copyright of the author / myArmoury; results paraphrased.

Test conditions (as stated):
- Mail: alternating rows of wedge-riveted and solid links, about 14 or 15 gauge wire, just under 7 mm inner diameter (a little over 6.5), by Julio Junco Funes. Comparison hauberk: imported Indian riveted mail, 9.5 mm ID.
- Gambeson under the mail: a discontinued cotton model by Revival Clothing (thick canvas shell, cotton batting), on a hanging pell.
- Jack: linen jack samples by Matuls, 10 and 20 layers; 30 layers = both stacked. Cuts were made on a pell hung from a chain (so it gives); thrusts on a pell leaned against a wall.
- Bow (for scale): compound bow at 50 and 70 lb, 400 grain arrows at 300 ft/s (factory figure), quoted as 108 J (a calculation by another forum member); field points.

Results, mail over gambeson:
- Longsword cut (Del Tin, MRL hilt, "mostly likely type XX"; powerful over-shoulder and overhead cuts): did not penetrate and broke no links. Some links were slightly bent and nicked; the edge damage was "extremely minor".
- Thrust (Albion Talhoffer, type XVa, half-sword grip, three thrusts, not at full force): one link broken. Point went about 5/8 in past the mail without a broken link and about 3 in with one. No photos of this part.
- Poleaxe cut (Arms and Armor Knightly Poleaxe): no full penetration, but at least 3 links broken per strike. Top-spike thrust: went through the mail and gambeson into the 4x4 post.
- Arrows: at 50 lb, all stopped at 15 ft and at 20 yd (at 15 ft, "some of the links were bent, one was broken", second check 2026-11-18). At 70 lb and 15 ft, 2 of 3 pierced. At 70 lb and 20 yd, all 3 stopped, each breaking at least one link. The 9.5 mm Indian hauberk did not stop the 50 lb bow at 20 ft.

Results, linen jack:

| Layers | Cut | Thrust |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Atrim 1508: through, small gash. Katana: through, massive gash. Brescia Spadona: "very good sized gash" | Rondel dagger: penetrated, not far. Katana: straight through. Atrim 1508 (half-sword) and Spadona (half-sword): through, stuck in the pell's wood |
| 20 | Atrim: more than half the layers. Albion Earl: about the same depth, longer gash. Brescia Spadona: all the way through (one-finger hole) | Atrim, Spadona (half-sword) and Earl (normal grip): total penetration |
| 30 | Atrim: only 2 layers. Spadona: about halfway. Earl: a couple more layers than the Atrim. Katana: completely through | Atrim (half-sword): failed, close. Spadona, Earl, Regent, Talhoffer (half-sword): penetrated. Rondel: only slightly. Katana (normal grip): penetrated, less easily |

Bow against the 10-layer jack: 3 of 3 stopped at 20 ft (70 lb).

## Sources disagree

- Measurement method: the Ensis Sub Caelo author finds the waggle test biased and unreliable without training and prefers pendulum tests. The maker PoB/CoP figures in section 3 give no method, so they cannot be compared directly with Pulaczewski's pendulum values.
- CoP position: the cutting swords in section 3 put the CoP at about 47 to 55 cm from the cross, which is 51 to 66% of blade length (computed by me). Pulaczewski's small-swords sit at 0.17 to 0.44 of blade length. His text says this is by design: cutting swords put it towards the tip, thrusting small-swords in the forte. A difference of type, not a conflict of data.
- Pulaczewski Table 2 lists IX 2804 twice ("IX 2804" and "IX2804", both 453 g), which looks like a duplicate in the source, so the 13-colichemarde mean (466 g, SD 78.7 g) may count one sword twice (second check, 2026-11-18).
