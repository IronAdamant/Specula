## Table J6-2. Grip strength across the life course (read directly)

Source: Dodds RM et al. (2014) "Grip Strength across the Life Course: Normative Data from Twelve British Studies", *PLoS ONE* 9(12): e113637, open access, CC BY ("distributed under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution License", © 2014 Dodds et al; confirmed by the second check in the Europe PMC full text, https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC4256164), https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0113637 (abstract and results).

Population: 60,803 observations from 49,964 participants (26,687 female) in 12 general-population studies in Great Britain, ages 4 to 90; Jamar, Smedley, Nottingham electronic and Takei dynamometers; most studies seated.

| Figure | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Peak median grip | 51 kg | 31 kg |
| Age range of the peak | 29 to 39 years | 26 to 42 years |
| Shape over life | rises to a peak in early adult life, holds through midlife, then declines | same |

The centile curves (10th to 90th by age) are in the paper's figures and supplementary tables; they were not transcribed in this pass.

## Table J6-3. Running: speed, ground force (read directly)

Source: Dorn TW, Schache AG, Pandy MG (2012) "Muscular strategy shift in human running", *J Exp Biol* 215: 1944–1956, https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/215/11/1944/10883/Muscular-strategy-shift-in-human-running. Licence: © The Company of Biologists; facts only.

Population: 9 experienced runners (5 men, 4 women), age 27.7 ± 8.0 y, mass 73.1 ± 8.6 kg, height 176 ± 7 cm, leg length 93 ± 5 cm; indoor synthetic track, Australian Institute of Sport. Not all ran every speed: N is 9, 9, 8 and 7 at the four target speeds (3.5, 5.0, 7.0 and 8.0 m/s or faster), so "9 runners" holds only for the two slower speeds.

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Measured steady speeds | 3.5 ± 0.1, 5.2 ± 0.1, 7.0 ± 0.1 and 9.0 ± 0.7 m/s (N = 9, 9, 8, 7) |
| Peak vertical ground reaction force | 2.7 body weights at 3.5 m/s, rising to 3.6 at 7.0 m/s, then no further change |
| Strategy | up to about 7 m/s runners speed up mainly by longer strides; above 7 m/s mainly by faster stride frequency |

Stride length, stride frequency and contact time per speed are in the paper's Table 2, which is an image on the journal page and was not transcribed.
