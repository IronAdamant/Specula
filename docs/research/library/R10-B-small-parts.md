## B1. Eye (adds to round two's B9)

**Iris diameter, measured as the white-to-white (WTW) corneal diameter (B-S1).** Population sample from Shahroud, Iran, aged 40-64; 4,787 people analysed, 58.1% female; right eyes only.

Table 1 percentiles in mm (5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 95th, 99th):

| Group | 5th | 25th | 50th | 75th | 95th | 99th |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Female | 11.03 | 11.47 | 11.77 | 12.03 | 12.46 | 12.78 |
| Male | 11.11 | 11.60 | 11.88 | 12.18 | 12.61 | 12.96 |
| Age 40-44 | 11.20 | 11.65 | 11.92 | 12.19 | 12.64 | 13.00 |
| Age 60-64 | 10.87 | 11.43 | 11.68 | 11.98 | 12.43 | 12.68 |

- Mean 11.80 mm. Normal range by mean plus or minus 2 SD: 10.8 to 12.8 mm. That implies an SD of about 0.5 mm (mine, (12.8 - 10.8) / 4).
- WTW falls 0.013 mm per year of age.
- Left and right eyes correlate at r = 0.801.
- Table 2 gives only means with 95% CIs, so no SD by sex. Use the percentiles instead.

**Fissure height and length.** Round two's B9 already covers this. One new adult row comes from R10-A source S19 (South Indian adults aged 18-26):
- width: men 31.08 (1.79) mm, women 29.90 (2.18) mm;
- height: men 11.30 (1.66) mm, women 11.58 (1.65) mm.
No open source gave fissure size by age band with SDs in this round. Gap.

## B2. Nail

**Jung et al. 2015 (B-S4).** 300 Korean volunteers with healthy nails: 150 men and 150 women, 30 of each sex in each decade from the 20s to the 60s; mean age about 45.
- Definitions (Fig. 1):
  - width is the greatest transverse distance between the lowest points of the lateral nail grooves;
  - length runs from the tip of the finger to the groove at the junction of the proximal nail fold and the eponychium. This is the visible plate including the free edge, measured to the fingertip.
- Text findings:
  - width, length, W/L ratio and the radius of transverse curvature are all larger in men and largest in the thumb;
  - in women, W/L is similar across the five fingers;
  - the radius of transverse curvature ranks thumb > index > middle > ring > little;
  - the dominant hand's nails are flatter (larger radius);
  - nails flatten with age;
  - height does not predict W/L or curvature.
- Per-finger width and length in mm (UNVERIFIED). Tables 1-5 are images that Europe PMC would not serve to this box. A search-engine summary of the same paper gives these values. Use them only as a placeholder until checked.

| Finger | Men W / L (mm) | Women W / L (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| Thumb | 15.2 (1.0) / 14.7 (1.0) | 13.2 (1.1) / 14.6 (1.1) |
| Index | 12.3 (1.0) / 13.0 (1.0) | 11.1 (1.1) / 12.1 (1.1) |
| Middle | 12.6 (1.0) / 13.4 (1.0) | 11.5 (1.1) / 12.6 (1.0) |

## B3. Breast and chest

**Women (B-S5).** 54 nulliparous Saudi women aged 20-25 with BMI 20-25. Mean (SD) in cm:
- sternal notch to nipple 19.8 (2.5), both sides;
- internipple distance 20.3 (2.3);
- nipple to inframammary fold: right 7.6 (1.6), left 7.8 (1.6), pooled 7.7 (1.6);
- lower areola edge to fold 5.4 (1.4);
- areola diameter 4.5 (1.4).

Left and right differ only for areola edge to fold. The discussion compares Chinese women (sternal notch to nipple 19.05 cm) and Turkish women (19.6 cm).

**Men (B-S6).** 150 cisgender men, median age 26, Amsterdam. Table 3, in cm:
- internipple distance 22.9 (2.0); the text says 23.0 (2.0);
- sternal notch to nipple 19.9 (1.7);
- sternal notch to umbilicus 38.7 (3.1);
- chest circumference 93.5 (8.0).

Fitted placement rules (abstract): NN = 4.11 + 0.035 x age + 0.041 x weight + 0.093 x CC + 0.140 x AUX-AUX; SNN = 7.248 + 0.303 x NN + 0.072 x weight. Bootstrapped R2 is 0.63 and 0.50. These give a conditional mean for male nipple position given body size. They also feed D and R10-MATH as a worked example of conditioning.

**Gaps:** base width and projection; any split by age or bra-size band. No open source with SDs was found in this round.

## B6. Fat map

**Sato and Demura 2009 (B-S11).** B-mode ultrasound at 14 sites: cheek, chin, triceps, two chest sites, subscapular, two back sites, abdomen, suprailiac, two thigh sites, knee, calf. 302 Japanese adults aged 20-83, split by sex, five age bands and obesity.
- Table 1, mean (SD), where the "plus or minus" sign is lost in the PDF text (mine):
  - non-obese: 107 men, BMI 19.8 (4.4); 67 women, BMI 21.2 (2.6);
  - obese (BMI 25 or more): 76 men, BMI 28.0 (2.9); 52 women, BMI 28.1 (2.7).
- Site means appear only in Fig. 1 (bar charts, no SDs).
- Figure readings (mine, about plus or minus 2 mm, p. 5 Fig. 1, 20s panels):

| Site | Non-obese men | Non-obese women | Obese men | Obese women |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abdomen | about 8 | about 9 | about 28 | about 11 |
| Thigh 1 | about 6 | about 10 | about 9 | about 9 |
| Triceps | about 6 | about 8 | about 8 | about 9 |
| Calf | about 5 | about 7 | about 7 | about 7 |

- The paper's own summary: fat moves toward the trunk with age and obesity; sex differences are clearest in the obese and in people aged 30-50.
- **Storchle et al. 2018 (B-S12).** Method for turning an 8-site ultrasound mean into a whole-body mean. Ten men with BMI 20.0-28.4; 216 random sites each.
  - The 8-site mean overestimates the body mean.
  - Multiply it by 0.65 (SD 0.05). The fit gives R2 of 0.95 with a standard error of 0.34 mm.
  - Segment means were highest at the buttocks (about 12 mm) and lowest at the hands (about 0.3 mm).
  - Use: if a generator sets site thicknesses, their mean times 0.65 should match the body's mean subcutaneous depth (mine).
- No open MRI table of subcutaneous thickness by sex and BMI band was found. A 7T MRI study of the lower leg (Diagnostics 2024, 14(20):2260, CC BY) reportedly gives calf fat 8.9 (3.7) mm in women and 4.3 (2.2) mm in men (UNVERIFIED, search summary only).
