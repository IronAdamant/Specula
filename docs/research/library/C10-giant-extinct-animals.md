# C10: Giant extinct animals: pterosaurs, sauropods and Tyrannosaurus, size, mass and speed estimates

Fixed after the second check (2026-11-18):
- *Quetzalcoatlus* mass: Henderson 2010's value, 544 kg, added (it was "not retyped"); the 200-250 kg estimate credited to Paul 2002 and Witton and Habib 2010 (not "Witton 2008"); the disagreement now reads 30 to 544 kg.
- *Argentinosaurus*: the other estimates on the same Wikipedia section added (Mazzetta 2004, González Riga 2016, Paul 2016 and 2019, Campione and Evans 2020); the disagreement now spans about 50 to 100 t.
- *T. rex* mass: Scotty (8.87 t), modern estimates (5.4 to 8.0 t) and historical averages (4.5 to over 7.2 t) added, so that Sue's 5.65 t is not read as the ceiling.

Locomotion argument (Witton and Habib 2010 abstract, read via Europe PMC 2026-11-17): bird and pterosaur launch kinematics are too different to be interchangeable; bone strength and flap-gliding performance show giant pterosaurs could fly; 10-11 m spans and 200-250 kg are the most reliable upper estimates, against earlier claims of 13 m and 544 kg.

## Sources disagree

- *Quetzalcoatlus* mass spans 30 to 544 kg across authors (Henderson 2010's 544 kg is the top end), driven mostly by assumed wingspan and bone density.
- *Argentinosaurus*: about 50 to 100 t across authors (Paul 2016's "over 50 t" to Paul 1994's 100 t and González Riga 2016's 96.4 t), with 83 t (volumetric) against 90-95 t (scaling); recent estimates cluster at 65-75 t.
- *T. rex* mass: Sue's 5.65 t (Erickson 2004) is not the ceiling: Scotty is estimated at 8.87 t, and modern estimates run 5.4 to 8.0 t.
- *T. rex* top speed: 4.5 to 20 m/s across studies.
