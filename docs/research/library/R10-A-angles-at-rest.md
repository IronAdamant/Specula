## A1. Ear

Sample (S1): 420 volunteers, 414 kept after excluding six under 15 (828 pinnas). Caucasian 69%, Afro-Caribbean 15%, Indian subcontinent 13%. Measured with calipers and a goniometer.

- Long axis tilt (measurement 2): mean 25 deg in males (range 15-35) and 27 deg in females (range 15-40). No difference by ethnic group (ages 15-59). No SD is printed for the axis.
- Protrusion, normal ears, both sexes, all ethnic groups, 765 pinnas (Table 5, "Normal" row), mean (SD):
  - Concho-mastoid angle: 45.0 (10.4) deg at the tragus level (6a); 31.1 (7.1) deg at the helical root (6b).
  - Conchal bowl depth: 18.0 (2.2) mm (7a); 18.5 (2.4) mm (7b).
  - Helical-mastoid distance: 18.9 (2.7) mm at the tragus level (8a); 20.0 (3.1) mm at the helical root (8b).
  - Whole-ear prominent ears (47 pinnas): angle 58.1 (10.6) and 47.9 (13.2) deg; distance 23.2 (2.5) and 27.5 (3.2) mm.
- The authors say angle, bowl depth and distance were continuous and normal, so they could not set a cut-off. Cut-offs they quote from others: Farkas, angle >40 deg; Adamson, helix-to-scalp upper limit 2 cm.
- Left against right (Table 2, correlations): ear length 0.96 (males), 0.94 (females); concho-mastoid angle 6a 0.72 and 0.78, 6b 0.79 and 0.55. Linear sizes are most symmetric; angle and bowl depth less so. The table header says Kendall's tau.
- Ear length by ethnicity, ages 15-29 (Table 3): Caucasian males 65.2 (4.2) mm, females 60.4 (3.2) mm. Caucasian ears lengthen 0.243 mm/yr (males) and 0.172 mm/yr (females).
- Missing from S1: helix-to-head distance at the top of the helix and at the lobe (only two levels are given: tragus and helical root), and ear height against the brow and nose base. I found no open source with SDs for these. A common sculpting rule (top of the ear near brow level, lobe near the nose base) is an artist's convention, not a measured norm.

## A2. Limbs

**Nguyen et al. 2009 (S2), Table 1.** Left leg, single examiner, college-aged, 102 males and 116 females. Standing measures used a stance with feet as wide apart as the acromions. Values are mean (SD) and minimum to maximum.

| Measure | Males | Females |
|---|---|---|
| Q angle (deg) | 9.3 (4.2), 2.0 to 21.3 | 13.5 (5.1), 3.3 to 31.3 |
| Pelvic angle, ASIS-PSIS to horizontal (deg; + = anterior tilt) | 9.0 (3.9), -1.0 to 19.0 | 12.1 (4.4), -1.7 to 24.0 |
| Femoral anteversion (deg, prone clinical method) | 8.6 (4.7), 0.3 to 23.3 | 14.7 (6.1), 3.7 to 35.0 |
| Tibiofemoral angle (deg; + = valgus) | 9.6 (2.7), 4.0 to 16.7 | 11.5 (2.5), 5.0 to 16.7 |
| Genu recurvatum (deg) | 3.2 (3.0), -2.0 to 13.0 | 5.6 (4.2), -2.0 to 21.0 |
| Tibial torsion (deg; + = outward) | 18.2 (7.0), 2.0 to 35.3 | 19.8 (6.9), -4.3 to 35.0 |
| Navicular drop (mm) | 6.2 (3.0), -2.0 to 15.3 | 6.8 (3.8), -2.7 to 18.0 |

- Table 2 (regression): each 1 deg of tibiofemoral angle adds 0.60 deg (males) or 0.62 deg (females) of Q angle. Each 1 deg of femoral anteversion adds 0.18 deg. The seven measures together explain 19.1% (males) and 17.9% (females) of Q-angle variance. This also feeds D4 and D5.
- Note: this "tibiofemoral angle" is a clinical surface angle. It is not the radiographic HKA angle.

**HKA angle (radiographic).** The best healthy-volunteer source I found is Hirschmann et al. 2019 (KSSTA, doi 10.1007/s00167-019-05507-1), but it is closed. From its abstract only (UNVERIFIED): 160 young adults (308 knees), HKA 179.2 (2.8) deg in males and 180.5 (2.8) deg in females. No open source with SDs was found.

**Carrying angle.**
- S3: 384 adults (214 men, 170 women), aged 20-74, goniometer confirmed by radiograph. Men: right 12.55 (2.3) deg, left 12.27 (2.43). Women: right 14.53 (2.34), left 14.06 (3.37). The angle is larger on the dominant side.
- S3 reports a mean age of 31.65 (3.76) years with an age range of 20-74. That SD is too small for that range (mine), so treat the age data with care.
- S4 (UNVERIFIED, search summary only): 346 Saudi adults aged 18-24 (181 men, 165 women). Men: right 7.98, left 7.79 deg. Women: right 13.21, left 13.03 deg. S4 shows a larger sex gap than S3. Both are reported as they stand.

## A3. Foot

- **Foot progression angle, walking (S5, Table 2).** GAITRite at midstance; 60 healthy adults (19 men, 41 women), mostly aged 18-29. FPA mean (SD): men 4.5 (5.6) deg, women 1.4 (5.4), all 3.3 (5.6). Positive means toe-out.
  - Same table: tibio-fibular torsion men 15.4 (5.6), women 17.8 (6.3) deg. Hip internal rotation correlates r = -0.40 with FPA and torsion r = 0.39.
  - In S5's Table 2 the "Total" hip ER (36.0) is above both the male (31.7) and female (35.7) means, which is impossible for a pooled mean (mine). Treat S5's table as lightly checked.
  - Standing foot angle: no open source with SDs was found. Gap.
- **Hallux valgus angle (S6, Table 2).** ROAD cohort, Japan; 1,996 people (654 men, 1,342 women), mean age 64.2. Radiographs taken supine (feet resting on the plate, not weight-bearing). HVA mean (SD):
  - all 16.8 (7.6); men 14.4 (6.2); women 18.0 (7.9) deg;
  - by age, men 13.6 (4.7) under 39 to 14.4 (7.2) at 80 and over; women 16.7 (4.8) to 17.9 (9.2).
  - IMA: all 8.6 (2.6) deg.
  - This is a general population, not healthy-only feet: 29.8% of feet had HV, which the study defines as HVA of 20 deg or more. For a healthy-feet prior (mine), truncate at 20 deg or take only the "normal grade" group.
- **Toe-tip line and toe types (S8).** More than 40,000 right feet, European population aged 1-70, 42% male; about 30% of body weight on the foot while scanned.
  - Toe types: Egyptian (big toe longest) 0.50, Roman (square) 0.38, Greek (second toe longest) 0.12. The sample has more children than adults.
  - The classifier fits two lines along the toe tips on feet normalised to 100 mm long by 38 mm wide. Greek if the line angle is under 0.02 rad and its length is at least 6 units. Roman if the angle is under 0.25 rad with length at least 12 units (looser pairs are also given).
  - Relative foot width (width/length) is about 0.45 at age 2, settling to about 0.38 in adults.
  - Each toe's length as a share of foot length with SDs: not found in an open source. Gap.
- **Arch height (S7).** 79 college-aged women, AHIMS device. AHI = dorsum height at half foot length divided by truncated foot length.
  - Seated: left 0.355 (0.031), right 0.369 (0.034).
  - Standing (loaded): left 0.338 (0.031), right 0.343 (0.033).
  - Loading lowers AHI by about 0.017-0.026 (mine, difference of means).
  - Arch height by arch type: S7 warns against typing feet from these values. No open table by type was found. Gap.

## A4. Trunk

- **Craniovertebral angle (S12, Tables 3, 5, 6).** Lateral photo, C7 marker to tragus against the horizontal (Kinovea); 122 healthy adults aged 18-75.
  - All 48.76 (6.77), min 32, max 64.
  - Men (61) 47.46 (6.71), 35 to 64. Women (61) 50.07 (6.63), 32 to 63.
  - By age band, pooled: 18-25: 52.55 (6.78); 26-35: 52.38 (5.15); 36-45: 49.45 (5.47); 46-55: 48.67 (6.23); 56-65: 46.55 (6.61); 66-75: 42.80 (5.54).
  - Table 7: falls 1.92 deg per decade unadjusted, 1.6 adjusted.
- **Spinal curves in adults by age and sex (S9, Tables 2, 3).** Standing radiographs; 1,461 adults (466 men, 995 women) aged 19-94. TK, LL and PT in deg, mean (SD), men / women:

- **Children (S11, Table 1).** 646 asymptomatic children (370 F, 276 M).
  - Ages 3-10: TK 42.0 (10.6), LL 53.8 (12.0), PI 43.7 (9.0), PT 5.5 (7.6), SS 38.2 (7.7).
  - Ages over 10 and under 18: TK 45.8 (10.4), LL 57.7 (11.1), PI 46.9 (11.4), PT 7.7 (8.3).
  - The "All" PT of 5.2 lies below both group means, which cannot be right for a pooled mean (mine). One cell has a typo; use the age-group rows.
  - Not split by sex.
- **Surface lordosis by age and sex, ages 5-85 (S10, Table 2).** Flexicurve; 2,497 people (1,264 women, 1,233 men). LLC mean (SD):
  - Women: 35.07 (10.32) under 10, 43.28 (12.41) at 11-19, 48.57 (11.23) at 20-29, rising to 50.52 (13.47) at 50-59, then 45.17 (16.65) over 70.
  - Men: 36.42 (10.61) under 10, 36.07 (11.23) at 11-19, 41.07 (12.87) at 20-29, 37.95 (9.51) at 60-69, 36.90 (12.11) over 70.
  - All women 46.01 (13.39), all men 38.57 (11.45).
  - The first and last band labels print as ">10" and "<70"; from the text they mean under 10 and over 70 (mine).
  - Surface Flexicurve values are not comparable with radiographic Cobb LL.
- **Pelvic tilt by sex.**
  - Clinical: ASIS-PSIS angle men 9.0 (3.9), women 12.1 (4.4) deg (S2).
  - Radiographic: PT, which is a different angle, in S9 above. The two are not interchangeable.
- **Shoulder slope (S13).** 156 Japanese women aged 18-25, 3D scan. Shoulder slope in three body-shape groups: 22.1 (4.2), 21.5 (4.4), 22.6 (4.0) deg (p. 5, Table 1). No male data and no pooled SD in S13. Gap for men.
- **Sternal angle (S14).** CT archive, 112 cases aged 0-50 (55 male, 57 female).
  - "Manubrio-sternal angle" men 27.79 (7.77), women 26.69 (6.77) deg. By age: 0-30 24.17 (7.19), over 31 29.29 (6.61).
  - These values look like the bend away from straight (180 minus the inner angle) (mine).
  - The abstract says the sex difference in this angle was significant, but Table 1 gives p = 0.427. The source contradicts itself.
  - The ranges in Table 2 are confidence intervals of the mean, not reference ranges (mine).
  - WEAK source: a patient archive that includes children.
  - A Folia Morphologica CT study (doi 10.5603/fm.a2018.0002) reportedly gives an inner angle of about 162-164 deg. UNVERIFIED (bot-check host).
- **Rib slope:** no open normative source with SDs found. Gap.

| Digit | MCP | PIP (thumb: IP) | DIP | Table |

- Supination flexes the PIP joints more. For example, the index PIP pooled over shoulder angles is 41 (13) in supination against 23 (8) in pronation.

- Thumb abduction and rotation at rest, and finger spread at rest: no open source with SDs found. Gap. S15 reports only the thumb's CMC flexion angle.

**S18.** 115 Turkish students aged 18-30 (56 men, 59 women). Values in deg:

  - S18: women 133, men 124.

**Palpebral slant (S19, Table 1).** 195 South Indian adults aged 18-26. Values are mean (SD):

- Fissure height: men 11.30 (1.66) mm, women 11.58 (1.65) mm.

**Not found as open normative values with SDs:**
- Gonial angle: found only in panoramic-radiograph dental studies whose hosts would not serve the PDF to this box. UNVERIFIED search-summary values (for example Indonesian men about 114 deg and women about 125 deg) are not used.
- Forehead slope angle.
- Chin projection as an angle.
- Brow height.
- Nostril axis on the basal view.
- Nasal tip rotation as a separate angle. Use the nasolabial angle as the proxy.

## Gaps (section A)
- Ear: helix-to-head distance at the top and at the lobe; ear height against the brow and nose base.
- Limbs: an open radiographic HKA source with SDs.
- Foot: standing foot angle; each toe's length as a share of foot length; arch height by arch type.
- Trunk: shoulder slope for men; rib slope.
- Hand: thumb abduction and rotation at rest; finger spread.
- Face: gonial angle (open copy); forehead slope; chin projection; brow height; basal nostril axis.
- S4 (Tedla) is UNVERIFIED.
