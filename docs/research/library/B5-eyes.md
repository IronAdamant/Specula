- Women blinked more than men, but the difference was significant only while reading.

## Not found

  SE). Bentivoglio measured rate, not duration. Choi 2003 (n = 36) is second-hand only.
- **Blink rate by population or ethnicity, and blink-rate SD.** Bentivoglio is a single Italian sample and reports

## Sources disagree

- **Bentivoglio vs itself:**
  - Reading change vs rest is −55.08% in the Results but "decreased by 74%" in the Discussion. The 74% probably
    describes a different measure, but the paper does not reconcile the two.
  - Conversation is +99.70% in the Results vs "100%" in the Discussion, which is only rounding.
- **Bentivoglio's own figure vs the norm it cites:** a resting rate of 17/min, against the earlier adult norm of
  19 ± 14/min.
- **Fissure height:**

  | Source | Fissure height (mm) |
  |---|---|
  | Kwon 2013 | 9.8 ± 0.2 SE (n = 25) |
  | Rana 2023 | 8.7 ± 1.3 SD (older cohort, mean 58 y) |
  | Kwon SH 2021, Korean women aged 20–39 | 9.16 ± 1.18 |
  | Fox 1966 | 7–13 (range) |

  Age and method (video vs photo vs 3D) explain some of the spread.
- **Upper lid position:**
  - Measured: 1–2 mm below the superior limbus (Rana intro).
  - Convention: MPEG-4 neutral face, "eyelids tangent to the iris", i.e. 0 mm of cover.
  - Use the measured value for realism and the MPEG-4 value only for compatibility with that standard.
- **Eye vs head onset:**
  - Unexpected targets: the eye leads by 25–40 ms.
  - Large or predictable shifts: the head can lead.
  - In cats, studies disagree on the sign: eye first by about 30 ms (Blakemore & Donaghy 1980) vs head first by about
    40 ms (Guitton 1984), both via Freedman.
  - Neck EMG leads eye EMG by about 20 ms, even when the eye moves visibly first.
- **Pupil range:** "2–8 mm" (Watson & Yellott) is a population envelope. The Winn age lines give about 3–8 mm across
  9–4400 cd/m², and older eyes are smaller at every luminance.
