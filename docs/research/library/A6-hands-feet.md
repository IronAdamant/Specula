The ratios I computed from Table I means (my arithmetic) agree to within rounding. For example, index: 22.38/15.82 = 1.41, 39.78/15.82 = 2.51, 68.12/15.82 = 4.31. The paper's Table III also gives the external finger length from the fingertip to each joint as a percentage of finger length (tip + distal phalanx: I 49.4%, II 27.8%, III 26.3%, IV 28.3%, V 33.9%; middle phalanx: II 31.0%, III 33.1%, IV 33.6%, V 32.0%; web height above the MCP joint: II 15.5%, III 15.3%, IV 18.5%, V 24.7%).

The paper reports that left and right lengths differ by no more than 0.5%.

**Combining with ANSUR (my suggestion, not a published method):** scale these bone lengths to a target hand length using the ANSUR hand length. Do not assume Buryanov's sample matches ANSUR in size, because Buryanov gives no stature or hand length.

## Sources disagree

- **Foot length / stature**: ANSUR gives 0.151 (F) and 0.154 (M), against Drillis–Contini's 0.152 (A1). They agree.
- **Hand length / stature**: ANSUR gives 0.111 and 0.110, against Drillis–Contini's 0.108 and de Leva's hand segment (A1). The definitions differ: ANSUR measures wrist crease to fingertip, while de Leva uses wrist joint centre to the third metacarpal or fingertip landmark.
- **Fibonacci finger proportions**: Buryanov & Kotiuk and Park et al. (as the former cite them) reject a single Fibonacci ratio across fingers. Ray V's middle phalanx is only about 1.1 × the distal one, against about 1.4–1.5 for rays II–IV.
