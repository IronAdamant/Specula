# A12 — Joint centres relative to surface landmarks

Accessed 2026-11-19.

## 1. Dempster 1955 — where to put joint centres on a living body

**Source.** Dempster, W.T. (1955), *Space Requirements of the Seated Operator: Geometrical, Kinematic, and Mechanical Aspects of the Body with Special Reference to the Limbs*, WADC Technical Report 55-159, Wright Air Development Center (US Air Force); DTIC AD0087892. Scan: https://archive.org/details/DTIC_AD0087892 (US government work). Report pages 123–125 (IA leaves n145–n147), read from the page images.
**Who.** The centres are a "suggested pattern for locating nominal joint centers on the living subject", worked out from **eight dismembered cadavers** (abstract, p. iii) plus joint studies on cadaver limbs and supplementary work on living subjects. Sex/age of the cadavers not restated on these pages (the report describes elderly cadaver material elsewhere; not checked this round).

| Joint centre | Landmark rule (quoted/paraphrased from p.124–125) |
|---|---|
| Sternoclavicular | Midpoint of the palpable junction between the clavicle's proximal end and the sternum at the upper border (jugular notch) of the sternum. |
| Claviscapular (AC region) | Midpoint of a line between the coracoid tuberosity of the clavicle (posterior border) and the acromioclavicular articulation; visualise it on the underside of the clavicle. |
| **Glenohumeral (shoulder)** | Midregion of the palpable bony mass of the head and tuberosities of the humerus; with the arm abducted about 45°, "a line dropped perpendicular to the long axis of the arm from the outermost margin of the acromion will approximately bisect the joint." |
| Scapular link | Distance between the claviscapular and glenohumeral centres: "approximately 3.5 cm" (called an unsatisfactory measurement). |
| Elbow | Midpoint of a line between the lowest palpable point of the medial epicondyle and a point **8 mm above radiale**. |
| **Wrist** | Palm side: the distal wrist crease at the palmaris longus tendon, or the midpoint between radial styloid and centre of the pisiform; back: the palpable groove between lunate and capitate, in line with metacarpal III. |
| Hand centre of gravity (rest) | On the palm, midway in the angle between the proximal transverse palmar crease and the radial longitudinal crease, in line with digit III. |
| Hip | Lateral view: a point at the tip of the femoral trochanter 0.4 inch anterior to its most laterally projecting part. |
| Knee | Midpoint of a line between the centres of the posterior convexities of the femoral condyles. |
| **Ankle** | "Level of a line between the tip of the lateral malleolus of the fibula and a point **5 mm distal to the tibial malleolus**." |
| Foot centre of gravity | Halfway along the oblique line from the ankle centre to the ball of the foot at the head of metatarsal II. |

**Centre-of-gravity positions along segments (Dempster Table 15, p.192; 16 segments observed except shoulder 14).** Hand (rest): 50.6% of wrist-axis-to-knuckle-III distance from the wrist axis. Forearm: 43.0% from elbow axis. Upper arm: 43.6% from glenohumeral axis. Shoulder mass: 71.2% of clavicular link from the glenohumeral axis (oblique). Foot: 24.9% of foot link from the ankle axis, 43.8% from heel, 59.4% from toe II (oblique); alternatively 42.9 : 57.1 along heel-to-toe.

## 2. Shoulder centre vs acromion in numbers

Dempster gives a rule, not a millimetre offset. **No numeric acromion-to-shoulder-centre offset was found in an open source.** Use Dempster's rule above (arm at about 45°, a perpendicular from the acromion's outer margin bisects the joint).

## 3. Ankle axis vs malleoli (cadaver measurement)

Isman R.E. & Inman V.T. (1969), "Anthropometric studies of the human foot and ankle", *Bulletin of Prosthetics Research* 10-11:97–129 (US Veterans Administration publication), https://rehab.research.va.gov/jour/69/6/1/97.pdf. **Who:** 46 cadaver legs, mostly older persons. From the text I could read (a partial copy at docslib.org): the empirical ankle axis "passed distal to the tips of the tibial and fibular malleoli". The numeric offsets in the paper's figures/tables were not readable: the VA server returned HTTP 500 on 2026-11-19, so they are **not copied here** (link only).

## 4. Finger joints vs knuckles

Greiner 1991 (A9) defines finger "links" between photographed joint landmarks, which gives segment spacing but not the depth of the joint centre below the dorsal knuckle. Dempster 1955 gives the wrist and hand c.g. only.

## Sources disagree

- Dempster's ankle centre (line from lateral malleolus tip to 5 mm below the medial malleolus) vs Isman & Inman's finding that the axis passes distal to both malleolar tips: both say "below the tips", but Dempster ties it to the lateral tip itself. Not resolved without Isman & Inman's numbers.
