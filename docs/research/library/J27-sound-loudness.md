## J27-1. Fall-off with distance (computed by me)

For a small source in open air, sound pressure falls as 1/r, so level falls 20 log10(r₂/r₁) dB: −6.0 dB per doubling of distance; −12.0 dB at 4×, −18.1 at 8×, −24.1 at 16×, −30.1 at 32×, −36.1 at 64×, −42.1 at 128×. (Two further statements that stood here, on air absorption and on line sources, are under Unconfirmed.)

## Sources disagree

- Threshold of pain is given as a range (120 to 134 dB) rather than a single value, and the range is not from one source: Wikipedia "Sound pressure" draws it from three references combined (Nave, HyperPhysics; NIOSH 1996 p. 88; Shure). The second checker could not read the HyperPhysics page itself.
- Krakatoa: the table gives 172 dB at 165 km, while the Brüel & Kjær quote the article also cites says 160 km (8.5 kPa, 172 dB).
- *Canadian Audiologist* balloon citation: dated 9 January 2014, but its URL path says volume 3, issue 6, 2016.
