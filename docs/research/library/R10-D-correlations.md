R squared for predicting the 8 dimensions left out of the PCA from the 10 in it (D-S1, Table III): maximum frontal breadth 0.83, bitragion chin arc 0.69, bitragion subnasale arc 0.66, neck circumference 0.66, bitragion frontal arc 0.61, head circumference 0.44 (rest cut off in my copy).

**A published boundary rule (D-S1, Appendix B).** Panel cells are drawn as ellipses in the PC1-PC2 plane: centre (281.6217618, 28.9865054), outer ellipse semi-axes 2.54 x 13.6991108 and 2.54 x 5.5847930, inner ellipse 1.21 x the same. Reading (mine): 13.699 and 5.585 are the SDs of the two PC scores, so the outer ellipse is a Mahalanobis radius of 2.54 in two dimensions. For a bivariate normal, the share inside radius k is 1 - exp(-k^2 / 2); k = 2.54 gives 0.960, which matches the paper's "more than 95%" coverage. This is the same idea as the chi-square bound in R10-MATH.

**3D landmark shape PCA (D-S2, p.37 and Table 11).** 28 landmarks after Procrustes alignment (size removed); 10 PCs explain 64.1%; PC1 14.5%, PC2 9.7%. Shape variation is spread thinly, unlike the size-dominated PCA above.

## D3. Hand (Greiner 1991, the 86 dimensions of round five A9)

Yes: Greiner has full correlation matrices and a PCA.

- **Correlation matrices**: Table 7 (men, pp. 218-243) and Table 8 (women, pp. 244-269), Pearson r for all dimensions except finger circumferences (those were predicted by regression, so not correlated). Significance thresholds after correction for 2,926 comparisons: |r| above 0.1347 for men, 0.1184 for women (p. 215).
- Excerpt, women, Table 8 p. 244 (page image): D1 length with D1 height 0.6290, with D1 tip to wrist 0.6528, with D1 link 0.7694, with D2 length 0.7182, with D3 length 0.6906; D1 tip to wrist with D1 metacarpal link 0.9007; D1 IP breadth with D2 length 0.2931 and with D2 PIP breadth 0.4092. Lengths correlate with lengths at about 0.6 to 0.8; breadths with lengths at about 0.2 to 0.4.
- **Regressions**: multivariate (pp. 270-273) and bivariate on hand length (digitised and measured) and hand breadth, by sex (pp. 274-285). Excerpt, women, Table 12 p. 282 (page image), predictor hand length measured (mm): D3 length = -1.89 + 0.44 x HL, SE 2.80, r squared 0.701; D3 height = 9.70 + 0.93 x HL, SE 3.61, r squared 0.865; D2 tip to wrist crease = 17.32 + 0.84 x HL, SE 4.37, r squared 0.782; D2 PIP joint breadth = 12.38 + 0.04 x HL, SE 1.20, r squared 0.103.
- **PCA** (pp. 376-377): 72 hand dimensions (text) reduced to 7 components with eigenvalue above 1, together 84.5%. PC1 eigenvalue 47.901, 65.6%; PC2 5.600, 7.7%; PC3 2.431, 3.3%. Table 17 gives each dimension's correlation with each component; for example joint breadths load highest on PCA1 (D2 PIP breadth 0.848, D3 PIP 0.862, D3 DIP 0.872) and metacarpal links on PCA2 (D3 0.782, D4 0.820).

Flags (mine): 47.901 / 72 = 66.5%, not 65.6%; 47.901 / 73 = 65.6%, so the PCA may have used 73 variables, not the 72 in the text. In Table 12, D4 tip to wrist crease shows slope 0.01 with r squared 0.781, which cannot give a value near 170 mm from an intercept of 4.51; probably a misprint (the neighbouring tip-to-wrist rows have slopes 0.84 to 0.94).

Reading (mine): in these data, joint angles are only weakly tied to segment lengths (Q angle was not predicted by femur-to-tibia ratio). Angles are tied more to neighbouring angles (scapula with humerus; FPA with hip rotation and tibial torsion). For generation, sample the angle block with its own correlations, then add length only through sex.

**Factor structure (D-S13, abstract).** 218 college-aged adults (102 men, 116 women). Three factors, the same on both sides and both sexes: (1) valgus alignment: anterior pelvic angle, Q angle, tibiofemoral angle; (2) pronated alignment: genu recurvatum, navicular drop, less outward tibial torsion; (3) femoral anteversion alone. About 60% of variance explained. The correlation matrices (Tables 3, 4) and loadings (Tables 5, 6) are images I could not read: UNVERIFIED.

## D5. Spine, pelvis and stance

**Adults (D-S11, Wakayama).** 1,461 people (466 men, 995 women), age 19-94, standing radiographs. Table 4 correlation matrix:

| | SVA | TK | PI-LL |
|---|---|---|---|
| SVA | 1 | | |
| TK | 0.12 | 1 | |
| PI-LL | 0.54 | -0.33 | 1 |

All three P < 0.001. Standardised partial regression on age: SVA 0.17, TK 0.30, PI-LL 0.23 (men); 0.29, 0.32, 0.23 (women).

**Exact identity.** PI = PT + SS (pelvic incidence equals pelvic tilt plus sacral slope), quoted in D-S12. This is geometry, not a correlation: a generator should sample two of the three and compute the third.

**Children (D-S12).** Mac-Thiong's scheme (Fig. 1, adapted from Eur Spine J 2007) links adjacent regions: moderate (0.3 to 0.5) and strong (0.5 or more) correlations from pelvis to lumbar to thoracic. LL tracks PI (Fig. 2, 646 normal children and adolescents). Exact r values are in the figure, not in my text copy.
