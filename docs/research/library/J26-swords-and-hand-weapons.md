## J26-4. Swing speed, energy and timing

- Sidesword vertical cut, measured from 30 fps video with the sword's mass, length and radius of gyration measured (Vincent, Ensis Sub Caelo, "Dissecting a cut", https://blog.subcaelo.net/ensis/dissecting-cut/): peak transverse speed 13.6 m/s at 0.27 s from the start of motion; edge speed at impact about 14 m/s with about 0.5 m/s along the edge; peak total kinetic energy 28 J; transverse energy at the blade 23 J. Stopping that in 5 cm of elastic target implies about 920 N peak force (author's computation). Hobbyist measurement, one cut, rolling-shutter error acknowledged.
- Longsword cuts filmed at 100 fps (Science and Fiction, "Cutting mechanics", https://www.science-and-fiction.org/swordfighting/cuts.html): explosive cuts last 250-350 ms from first motion to impact; standard cuts with a step 600 ms or more and gain 10-20 cm of reach; angular speed at impact 1,000-2,000 °/s; the constant-speed arc lasts under 50 ms; computed energies up to about 330 J (including the arms); point-in-line cuts arrive 25-35 ms later than cuts through. Same page's comparisons: world-class javelin about 360 J, 170 lb longbow about 130 J, Roman pilum about 105 J. Hobbyist measurement.
- Iaido swing sensor study reports about 17 m/s tip speed (Evaluation of Skills in Swing Technique in Classical Japanese Swordsmanship in Iaido Using Sensors, *J. Mech. Eng. Autom.* 2016, doi:10.17265/2159-5275/2016.04.004; seen in a search summary only, not opened).

Game use: a cut is a wind-up (body), a 30-40° acceleration arc, a fast constant-speed arc (under 50 ms), then a deceleration arc; total 0.25-0.6 s.

## Sources disagree

- Longsword mass: 1.8-3 kg (Wikipedia infobox) against 1.13-1.53 kg for the Met's 15th- and 16th-century long-gripped swords (J26-1).
- Cut energy: 23-28 J at the blade (sidesword, Ensis Sub Caelo) against about 330 J (longsword with arms, Science and Fiction). They measure different things: blade-only transverse energy against whole moving system.

## Not found

- Balance point and moments of inertia for the Met swords (not in the records; Peter Johnsson and others publish such data for a few swords, not found as open data).
- Peer-reviewed motion-capture tables of longsword strike speed (a 2022 EUROCAST dataset paper, doi:10.1007/978-3-031-25312-6_59, was seen in search only).
- Measured armour penetration energies for mail and plate from an open primary paper (Williams, *The Knight and the Blast Furnace*, is not open).
- Two-handed sword weights in the Met sample (none came back flagged with a weight under that object name).
