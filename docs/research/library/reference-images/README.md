# Reference pictures: the list

What [REFERENCE_IMAGES_BRIEF.md](../../REFERENCE_IMAGES_BRIEF.md) asked for, gathered by Grok at the
owner's request and delivered on 2026-11-17 in three parts ("Reference images supplied. Add them to the
library"). The owner's reason, the same day: "it's how human artists, in any profession, work. They
refer to skeleton images before putting in their touches... Even though they know what humans look
like, they still use reference and measurements."

- **[INDEX.md](INDEX.md) is the delivery's own index, kept as it came.** A row a picture: what it shows,
  the view, the subject, the page it came from with its author, the licence, the date it was checked.
  It also says how well each row of the brief is covered and which pictures were found and not taken.
- **The pictures themselves are not in this repository yet.** There are 110 of them (211 MB), all from
  Wikimedia Commons: on the development machine they are in `gallery-full/reference-images/`, laid out
  as the index names them (`1-whole-body/`, `2-weak-areas/`, `3-head/`, `4-motion/`, `5-creatures/`).
  Anyone can fetch any of them from the link in its row. Whether the files (or small copies of them)
  are to be committed here is the owner's to say: see HANDOFF, "Open owner decisions".

## Rights, as the index gives them

88 pictures are in the public domain, 3 are CC0, and 19 are CC BY (18 under 4.0, one under 2.0), each
with its author in its row. None is non-commercial or no-derivatives. Every licence was read from the
Commons record for the file, not guessed; three pictures whose licence was unclear or which were too
small were left as links.

The index's first paragraph says the pictures are "not to be traced, copied into a repository, or
shipped with the engine": that is the brief's own rule, written before the owner's word above. The
rule that stands whatever is decided about the files: **they are looked at and compared with the
engine's captures, never traced, and no picture's pixels go into an asset.**

## What is there, and what is not

Well covered: the adult male body, front, side and back, as skeleton, as muscles and as a living figure
(Albinus, Richer, Vesalius); neck and shoulders; hands; feet; knees; hips and the pelvis of each sex;
skulls and a living male head; walking and running (Muybridge), and four animals' gaits; creatures with
a skeleton each: dog, lion, horse, cow, goat, deer, bear, elephant, ostrich, bat, pterosaur, lizard,
crocodile, snake.

Thin or missing, by the index's own account: a woman's whole figure from the side, and women in general
beside the men; a whole figure at three-quarter; slender and heavy builds; a living female head and a
range of ages; elbows a little; **fish, spiders and insects are missing**.

Some of the motion plates and living figures are unclothed academic studies of the 1880s and 1890s.
