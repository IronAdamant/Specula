The pattern: legs grow fastest in mid-childhood, so upper leg / height peaks around ages 14–19. In older adults the ratio falls and arm / height rises. That is consistent with loss of stature (mostly trunk and spine) while long bones keep their length. Adult height by age band (NHANES 2017–2018, weighted mean ± SD, my computation): men 175.9 ± 7.2 (20–39), 175.8 ± 7.5 (40–59), 173.4 ± 7.2 (60–80); women 162.6 ± 6.7, 161.4 ± 6.8, 158.8 ± 6.5. These are cross-sectional, so birth-cohort effects are mixed in with ageing.

## Sources disagree

- **WHO vs CDC references**: not compared in this pass, because the CDC files could not be fetched.
- **Head height / stature in late adolescence**: Snyder/WHO gives about 0.124–0.127, against Drillis–Contini's adult 0.130. That is close, but Drillis gives no sample or definition (see A1).
- **Leg proportion definitions**: NHANES "upper leg length" (a seated thigh measure) is not the subischial leg length used in the sitting-height-ratio literature, so the ratios are not interchangeable.
