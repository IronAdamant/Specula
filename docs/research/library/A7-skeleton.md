## A7-5. Rib cage

- **On the living body (ANSUR II, over flesh)**: chest breadth F 26.93 ± 1.86, M 28.94 ± 1.83 cm (pp. 89–90); chest depth F 24.74 ± 2.73, M 25.38 ± 2.62 cm (pp. 93–94), https://tools.openlab.psu.edu/publicData/ANSURII-TR15-007.pdf (accessed 2026-11-16). Both are taken at "chest point, anterior", which for women is the bust point, so they are not skeletal. Depth/breadth per person (computed, https://tools.openlab.psu.edu/publicData/ANSUR_II_FEMALE_Public.csv (accessed 2026-11-16) and https://tools.openlab.psu.edu/publicData/ANSUR_II_MALE_Public.csv (accessed 2026-11-16)) is F 0.918 (SD 0.076) and M 0.877 (SD 0.075).
- **Rib cage height cue**: from suprasternale (0.816–0.819 H) down to the tenth rib (0.638–0.648 H), so the visible front of the rib cage spans about 0.17–0.18 of stature (my arithmetic from Table A7-1).
- **Shape change with age (qualitative only)**: Weaver A.A., Schoell S.L., Stitzel J.D. (2014). Morphometric analysis of variation in the ribs with age and sex. *J Anat* 225(2):246–261. DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/joa.12203 (accessed 2026-11-16); PMC https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4111931/ (accessed 2026-11-16) (blocked from the box; the abstract was read via Europe PMC, https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1111/joa.12203&resultType=core&format=json (accessed 2026-11-16)). The study used CT scans of 339 people aged 0–100. In my words: from birth through adolescence the cage grows, the thoracic curve flattens, and the ribs tilt downward relative to the spine. From young adulthood into old age the thoracic curve increases again, the ribs rotate upward toward horizontal, and the cage becomes rounder. The abstract gives no numeric rib angles.

## Sources disagree

- **Subpubic angle varies by method**: keep the methods apart. The 3D CT and dry-bone subpubic figures in Table A7-2b are about 64–75° (M) and 83–113° (F). These are separate from Kolesova & Vētra’s CT canal-diameter figures in Table A7-2a. The AP-radiograph studies in Mohd Ali Table 4 give 75.8–101.5° (M) and 93.5–140.0° (F), including Young & Ince 75.8° / 93.5°. Oladipo’s “projection images” rows (100.1–105.6° M / 119.4–125° F) are a further, separate method and are not folded into the AP-radiograph range. Use CT or bone values for skeletal geometry.
- **Kolesova female sample size**: n = **212** is the best-supported value (abstract and Table 1; back-calculation of the paper’s own t statistics also fits 212, not 122). The 122 in the methods text is a typo. No erratum was found. The authors’ 2017 paper on the same Gailezers archive and period reports 211 females (Kolesova, Kolesovs & Vetra, *Anat Cell Biol* 50:265–274, https://doi.org/10.5115/acb.2017.50.4.265 (accessed 2026-11-16)).
- **Facial tissue depths by method**: Hona & Stephan report that cephalograms and CT give larger depths and needle puncture gives the smallest, so the pooled means blend methods.
- **ANSUR chest depth vs skeletal thorax**: these are not comparable, because ANSUR measures at the bust point.
