# B9 Face, teeth, eye and ear dimensions by sex and ancestry

Fixed after the second check (2026-11-18): Cho's right ear is within 0.6 mm of the left except for auricle distance from the inferior orbital rim in men (1.3 mm); Cho's ear data are now stated to be 3D-CT soft-tissue measurements of hospital patients; the reading that the older surveys used callipers is moved under Unconfirmed; Europe PMC URLs are added to every source, which gave DOI links only. Notes added from the checker: Cho Table 6's wrong footnote, the age at which men's ear length peaks, and that Uslu-Akcam's 10 to 26 years include adolescents.

Round 4, gap 7. All values were retyped from tables in open-access full texts, read through Europe PMC fullTextXML. Units are mm unless stated; values are mean +/- SD unless stated. Each study gives one population, so ancestry comparisons come from setting studies side by side or from a paper's own comparison table (marked SECONDARY when the paper relays other studies). Selected rows only, never a whole table.

## 1. Facial anthropometry: Malaysia (Malay, Chinese, Indian and others), adults 18+

Source: Lim YC, Abdul Shakor ASA, Mohamad N, Pahrol MA, Ismail R, Chong ZL, Abdul Mutalip MH, Omar MA, Danaee M, Wan GT, Shaharudin R (2022) "Head and face anthropometric study for respirators in the multi-ethnic Asian population of Malaysia". Front Public Health 10:972249. doi:10.3389/fpubh.2022.972249. PMC9459016. Licence CC BY 4.0 (read from the article).

Method: 3,324 participants from the National Health and Morbidity Survey 2020, with a complex survey design. Callipers for the breadths; 2D photogrammetry for the rest (Table 1).

IMPORTANT on spread: the means and SDs are "Weighted to represent population in Malaysia" (Table 3 footnote). The weighted SDs are very small (for example bizygomatic breadth SD 1.1) and do not describe person-to-person spread; the 5th to 95th percentiles in Table 3 do. Use the percentiles for spread.

## Sources disagree

- Malaysian weighted SDs (for example bizygomatic 141.5 +/- 1.1) against the US and Chinese SDs in the same Table 5 (+/- 4.7 to 6.9). The Malaysian figures are survey-weighted and behave like standard errors (my reading). The Table 3 percentiles (P5 120 to P95 156 mm) show a spread like the other studies.
- Nose breadth: Malaysian men 43.2 vs US men 36.6 and Chinese men 39.2 (Lim et al. Table 5). The delivery read part of this as a difference of method; that reading is under Unconfirmed.
- Korean ear SDs (about 10 mm for ear length) are 2 to 3 times those of the relayed Indian, Caucasian and African samples (about 3 to 4 mm). The Korean values are 3D-CT soft-tissue measurements of hospital patients (second check, 2026-11-18), which may bear on this. The Korean values also include implausible extremes (lobular width SD 16.7 in men 70+). Treat the Korean spread with caution.
- Turkish tooth 42 MD (female): minimum 1.27 mm against a mean of 6.073; almost certainly a data-entry error in the source (my reading). The SD 0.603 is inflated by it.
- Upper central incisor MD: Turkish F 8.772 / M 8.984 vs Iranian F 8.626 / M 8.789. This is a small population and age-range difference, not a conflict.

## Not found

- Normative adult palpebral fissure length and height by sex from an opened open-access source (only a South Indian children's study came up, PMC11282901, not used: children).
- Facial anthropometry from Farkas, or Zhuang 2010 by ethnicity, opened directly (only the values relayed by Lim et al. are given).
- Tooth crown heights (cervico-incisal) by sex.
- Axial length by ancestry in a single multi-ethnic study.
- African and European ear data from an opened primary source.

## Unconfirmed (second check, 2026-11-18)

- That the older US and Chinese surveys relayed in Lim et al. Table 5 (Zhuang and Bradtmiller 2005; Du et al. 2008) measured with callipers, so that part of the nose-breadth difference may be method. Lim does not say how those surveys measured, and neither the researcher nor the checker opened those papers: it is an untested reading. **Not to be used until a source that gives the method has been opened.**
