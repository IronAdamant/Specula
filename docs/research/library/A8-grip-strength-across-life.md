# A8: Grip strength across the life course (body sets: children, adults, older people)

Researched 2026-11-17 (all URLs accessed 2026-11-17). Brief section 2, "children and old people", and section 3, "Human body". Proportions by age (WHO growth standards, NHANES limb fractions) are already in the main library's A2; this file adds strength by age.

## A8-1. Normative grip strength, Great Britain

Source: Dodds R.M. et al. (2014), "Grip strength across the life course: normative data from twelve British studies", *PLoS ONE* 9(12): e113637, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0113637, licence CC BY (Europe PMC record). Abstract read 2026-11-17 via https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/ ; centile curves are in the paper's figures and tables (not retyped).

What was measured: hand-held dynamometer grip (maximum of trials), 60,803 observations from 49,964 people (26,687 female) in 12 general-population studies, ages 4 to 90.

| Quantity | Males | Females |
|---|---|---|
| Peak median grip | 51 kg | 31 kg |
| Age range of the peak | 29-39 years | 26-42 years |
| "Weak grip" (at least 2.5 SD below the sex-specific peak mean) prevalence at age 80 | 23% | 27% |

Shape of the life curve (same source): rise to a peak in early adult life, plateau through to midlife, decline from midlife onwards; males stronger than females from adolescence.

Converted (computed by me, g = 9.81 m/s²): 51 kg-force = 500 N; 31 kg-force = 304 N.

## A8-2. Grip geometry

Optimum grip cylinder: 1 to 3 in (2.5 to 7.6 cm) diameter for a full wrap grip (Wikipedia "Grip strength"; the statement carries no citation, so treat as unsourced). See I4-2 for stair handrail heights.
