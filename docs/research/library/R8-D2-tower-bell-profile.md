## D2-1. Grimthorpe's construction for a large bell on the "13 scale" (S1 pp. 357-359, Fig. 80)

### The unit

- Divide the **mouth diameter D into 24 equal parts**. All measures below are in parts (1 part = D/24).
- The rule is meant for large bells on the 13 scale of thickness (D-2 below). Thicker bells need only a small change to the outside curve.

### Inside surface ("inside sweep")

- The inside is a **quarter ellipse**: semi-major axis AC = 14 parts (vertical) and semi-minor axis CB = 6 parts (horizontal).
- A is the inside of the lip, at the mouth. C lies 14 parts directly above A, and B lies 6 parts in from C, towards the bell's axis.
- Grimthorpe draws it with a string and two pins. The foci S and H are found by striking radius 14 from B onto the line AC (extended).
- **(mine)** For an ellipse, the distance from the end of the minor axis to each focus is the semi-major axis, so the foci are √(14² − 6²) ≈ 12.65 parts from C.
- **(mine)** A sits 12 parts from the axis (E is the centre of the mouth). So the inside radius is 12 at the mouth and 6 at the top of the sweep: **the inside diameter at the shoulder is ½ D**, reached 14 parts (0.583 D) above the mouth.

### Thicknesses

- **Sound-bow** PQ (the thickest part, where the clapper should strike) = **D/13** (about 1.85 parts).
- **Waist** = ⅓ of the sound-bow = **D/39**, which Grimthorpe rounds to "practically 2 thirds of a part".
- The construction gives a thickness of 1 part at 8 parts along from A.

### Outside surface

- Built from several curves "in some empirical way". In order:
  1. **Upper outside.** A second quarter ellipse with centre c, its minor axis placed ½ part below CB. Its end b is ⅔ part outside B (the waist thickness). The foci s and h are struck with radius 11 from b, so **(mine)** its semi-major axis is 11. Only the upper part, from R to b, is used.
  2. **Sound-bow.** Draw the line from s through Q and P to the point 4 parts along the base, and set PQ = D/13.
  3. **Lip.** A circular arc AQ of radius 3½ to 4 parts ("hardly any difference"). Its centre K falls wherever it falls.
  4. **The short piece QR** is faired in by hand.
- **Figure label (mine).** At the Gutenberg figure's resolution the semi-minor axis cb reads "5 2/5". The text implies 6 − ⅔ = 5⅓. The difference (0.07 part) is below drawing accuracy, but I cannot tell which was meant.

### Height and crown

- The lowest bells are a little taller than the inside elliptic quadrant; the extra height is added as a straight cylinder.
- The **top (crown)** is a circular arc of radius **16½ to 18 parts** centred at E, the centre of the mouth. The figure uses 17.
- Small and thick bells are usually made taller than large ones.
- **(mine)** So the crown is about 17/24 ≈ **0.71 D** above the mouth along the axis (range 0.69-0.75 D).
- Grimthorpe calls the extra height of foreign bells, "beyond the height of 18 parts", a waste of metal in large bells (p. 360).

### Hanging and clapper (p. 359)

- The pit (the frame space a swinging bell swings in) must be **at least 5/3 of the diameter**, and longer for tall bells or bells hung long.
- The clapper strikes the thickest part of the sound-bow. Its "flight" (tail) and its pivot must sit below the gudgeons (the pivots the bell swings on).

## D2-2. Weight and thickness scales (S1 pp. 343-345)

- **"13 scale":** the mouth diameter is about **13 x the sound-bow thickness**. This is about the *least* weight a good bell should have: 72 cwt for a 72-inch (6 ft) bell, or 9 cwt for 3 ft. Such a bell is nearest B flat at Grimthorpe's proposed pitch (A = 880).
- **"12 scale":** most great European bells are heavier, averaging about 4 tons for 6 ft (10 cwt for 3 ft), and sound about a note higher.
  - The Westminster bells are nearly on this scale (D ≈ 12 x the thickness).
  - Great Paul is heavier still and taller for its width.
- **(mine)** Weight follows D³ at a fixed scale. Grimthorpe compares weights against the cubes of diameters in his list of great bells.
- **Note intervals (p. 343):** bells 1½ notes apart weigh about 58 : 100; half a note apart, about 5 : 6.
- **Old practice (p. 345):** old founders made the large bells of a peal somewhat thinner and the small ones thicker.
  - Example: the Exeter tenor is only D/15 at the sound-bow, but its waist is 2 in, or D/36, not the D/45 some modern founders use.
  - Grimthorpe holds that no sound-bow should be as thin as D/15.

## D2-3. Patterns compared (S1 p. 360)

- **Grimthorpe/Westminster pattern:** inside ellipse with semi-major 14. Fuller outside at the sound-bow, so a straight edge laid against the lip and shoulder is pushed out by the sound-bow.
- **Usual older English pattern:** a "more hollow" ellipse with semi-major 12. A straight edge touches both lip and shoulder.
- **Foreign (continental) pattern:** not an ellipse; much *less* hollow, does not end horizontally at the mouth, and is taller.
- **Shape and size (pp. 355-356):**
  - Hemispherical bells work only up to about 7 in.
  - Hand bells and small bells should be thinner and more conical.
  - Above about 2 ft diameter the standard church-bell shape suits every size from 4 cwt to the largest Russian bell (about 220 tons).

## D2-4. The older rule of thumb, which disagrees (S2 p. 687)

S2 states:
- bell metal is copper and tin in about 4 : 1;
- the edge (lip) thickness is about **one-tenth of the diameter**;
- the **height is twelve times that thickness**.

**(mine)** That makes height ≈ 1.2 D and lip thickness D/10. Both are far from Grimthorpe:

| | S2 | S1 |
|---|---|---|
| Height | about 1.2 D | about 0.71 D |
| Thickness | D/10 at the lip | D/13 at the sound-bow (D/12 on the heavy scale) |

S2 gives no source or figure for this rule. It may measure "height" along the slanting side or include canons (the hanging loops on the crown); the article does not say. I would treat S1's construction as primary and S2 as a heavier, taller outlier.

## Summary of ratios for a shape library (mine, from S1)

| Quantity | Ratio to mouth diameter D |
|---|---|
| Sound-bow (max) thickness | 1/13 (13 scale), 1/12 (heavy scale); old thin bells 1/15 |
| Waist thickness | 1/39 (⅓ of the sound-bow); old practice 1/36 to 1/45 |
| Inside diameter at the top of the sweep (shoulder) | ½ |
| Height of the inside sweep | 14/24 = 0.583 |
| Crown height (arc radius from the mouth centre) | 16.5/24 to 18/24 = 0.69 to 0.75 |
| Lip arc radius | 3.5/24 to 4/24 = 0.146 to 0.167 |
| Swing pit length | ≥ 5/3 |

## Known problems

- S1's construction is explicitly empirical ("in some empirical way"; QR "filled up by hand"), and Grimthorpe says the details are left to the founder.
- Modern founders (Taylor, Whitechapel) use "true-harmonic" profiles (tuned so the bell's partial tones are in harmony) that are not published as open constructions. I found no open modern profile table in this pass.
- The cb = 5⅖ against 5⅓ label question above.
- S2's ratio is unsourced and conflicts with S1.
