## Table J5-1. Tyre rolling resistance coefficient (RRC, read directly)

RRC is rolling resistance force divided by wheel load. Source: Transportation Research Board, *Special Report 286: Tires and Passenger Vehicle Fuel Economy* (2006), Chapter 3, https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11620/chapter/5. Read free online at the National Academies Press.

| Figure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Typical range, new US passenger tyres | 0.007 to 0.014 | SR 286 ch. 3: "For most passenger tires sold in the United States, the coefficient of the tire measured when it is new falls between 0.007 and 0.014." https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11620/chapter/5 |
| Worked example in the report | 1,000 lb load gives 7 to 14 lb rolling resistance per tyre | same |
| EPA test of 36 radial tyre lines | 0.0098 to 0.0138, mean 0.0113 | SR 286 ch. 3, Table 3-2 discussion |
| Michelin, 24 original-equipment tyres for MY1995 vehicles | 0.0077 to 0.0114 | SR 286 ch. 3, Table 3-3 discussion |
| Goodyear estimate, new OE tyres | 0.0067 to 0.0152 | SR 286 ch. 3 |
| Goodyear estimate, new replacement tyres | 0.0073 to 0.0131 | SR 286 ch. 3 |
| Effect on fuel use | a 10% cut in RRC gives about 1 to 2% better fuel economy | SR 286 ch. 3 |
| Share of mechanical energy at the wheels lost to rolling resistance | about one-third | SR 286 ch. 3 |

All RRCs above were measured on new tyres under SAE J1269 unless the report says otherwise (SR 286 ch. 3).

## Table J5-2. Car drag coefficients

See J4-1 (OpenStax Table 6.2): Camry 0.28, Focus 0.32, Civic 0.36, Testarossa 0.37, Ram pickup 0.43, Hummer H2 0.64, bicycle 0.90. OpenStax adds that "at highway speeds, over 50% of the power of a car is used to overcome air drag" (https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/6-4-drag-force-and-terminal-speed). Frontal areas for these cars are not given by OpenStax.
