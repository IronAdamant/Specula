## F1. Dog (F-S1)

**Sample.** 1,155 purebred dogs from 109 breeds (results), 27 body measurements (31,185 in all; about 40 judged likely errors and handled, Methods). The acknowledgements thank owners who measured their own dogs or let the team measure, so not every dog was measured by the authors. Table 1 of the paper defines the measures, including withers height, limb segments and girths.

**Withers height, mean plus or minus 1 SD, inches, by sex (Table 2, breeds with a written height standard).** Columns: breed, male, female. cm = inches x 2.54 (mine).

| Breed | Male (in) | Female (in) | Male SD as % of mean (mine) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Borzoi | 31.33 +/- 1.2 | 28.28 +/- 1.6 | 3.8 |
| Great Dane | 34.97 +/- 2.4 | 32.32 +/- 1.3 | 6.9 |
| Standard Poodle | 25.09 +/- 1.2 | 23.64 +/- 1.1 | 4.8 |
| Beagle | 13.75 +/- 0.4 | 13.02 +/- 1.1 | 2.9 |
| Irish Wolfhound | 34.58 +/- 0.9 | 31.96 +/- 1.4 | 2.6 |
| Rottweiler | 25.33 +/- 0.5 | 22.85 +/- 1.1 | 2.0 |
| Whippet | 21.36 +/- 1.3 | 19.89 +/- 1 | 6.1 |
| Mastiff | 31.6 +/- 1.1 | 27.47 +/- 1.3 | 3.5 |

**Range.** Individual withers heights run from 6.75 in (a 12-year-old female dachshund) to 38.5 in (a 4-year-old male great Dane) (results text). That is the whole-species range across breeds, not a within-breed range.

**Structure.** The PCA on all 1,155 dogs gives PC1 = overall size (all loadings the same sign) and PC2 = long thin limbs against short thick limbs and broad chest, head and neck. Achondroplastic breeds (basset hound, corgis, dachshund and others) sit at the low end of PC2 (results and Fig. legend).

**Reading (mine).** Within a breed, the withers SD is about 2-7% of the mean, which is the same order as human stature (about 4%). Breed, not individual spread, makes up almost all of the variation in dogs. So a generator should pick the breed template first (round 9 D2) and then add a small within-breed spread.

**Tab. 2, limb indices (%), mean (SD).** Males: brachial 96.84 (3.64), crural 105.79 (6.50), IMI 89.88 (3.40), HFI 94.03 (5.81). Females: 96.35 (3.44), 107.70 (6.80), 89.77 (2.99), 95.00 (4.95). Total: 96.65, 106.55, 89.83, 94.42. Sex differences in the indices are not significant (NS).

**Flag (mine).** The discussion gives IMI as "89.93", but Tab. 2 gives 89.83 for the total. I use the table value.

**Reading (mine).** Bone length CVs are about 6-8%. The index SDs (about 3-7 points) are much smaller in relative terms. So the proportions stay steady while overall size varies, which fits a model of "one size factor plus small proportion noise". The cat figures are bone lengths (skeleton), not outer limb segments.

## F3. Horse (F-S3, F-S4)

### Standing joint angles, Morna racehorses (F-S3)

**Sample.** 50 standing horses of the Morna breed, Pakistan. Age 4.81 +/- 0.67 years, body weight 350.42 +/- 15.56 kg (Table 1). Angles were measured on the standing horse with a protractor, goniometer and height stick, between lines drawn through named joint centres (Methods 2.2-2.3, Fig. 1-2).

| Angle | Mean +/- SD (degrees) |
|---|---|
| Shoulder joint | 87.57 +/- 2.94 |
| Shoulder versus ground | 58.38 +/- 2.02 |
| Elbow | 123.02 +/- 3.46 |
| Knee (carpus) | 171.52 +/- 2.39 |
| Fore fetlock | 147.68 +/- 5.11 |
| Fore pastern versus ground | 60.80 +/- 3.53 |
| Hip joint | 85.82 +/- 2.62 |
| Stifle | 128.62 +/- 4.08 |
| Hock | 160.40 +/- 3.89 |
| Hind fetlock | 155.48 +/- 2.68 |
| Hind pastern versus ground | 60.28 +/- 4.01 |

Sources: abstract and Results 3.2 (elbow, knee, fetlocks, stifle, hock) and 3.3 (shoulder, hip, against-ground angles). The angles did not correlate significantly (p > 0.05) with age, body weight or body condition score. The discussion compares them with other breeds: hip 88 degrees in Thoroughbreds and 99 in French trotters; hind fetlock 157 in jumping Thoroughbreds and 142 in French trotters. These are secondary values from the discussion; I did not check them against the cited papers.

**Caveat (mine).** Surface-landmark angles on a standing horse depend on the stance and on skin moving over the bone, which the authors also note. They suit setting a rest pose, not a joint model.

### Body and limb segments, Polish Konik (F-S4)

**Sample.** 172 Polish Koniks (46 male, 126 female) aged 3 to 24 years, from five breeding centres; 40 measurements in cm (Methods). Table 1 gives mean +/- SD per centre plus a "Total" column. Sexes are not reported separately.

**Selected rows, Table 1 (cm).** Centre columns: Sierakow (n=30), Kobylniki (n=48). The SD range is across all five centres (mine).

| Trait | Sierakow | Kobylniki | Within-centre SD range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Withers height | 135.8 +/- 1.97 | 133.4 +/- 2.96 | 1.97-3.88 |
| Head length | 48.9 +/- 2.56 | 47.6 +/- 2.57 | 1.78-3.39 |
| Thorax circumference | 166.4 +/- 4.96 | 173.5 +/- 6.29 | 4.96-8.01 |
| Scapular length | 56.0 +/- 2.04 | 56.9 +/- 1.73 | 1.40-2.14 |
| Forearm length | 37.6 +/- 2.12 | 36.3 +/- 3.02 | 1.46-3.02 |
| Metacarpal circumference | 18.0 +/- 0.64 | 19.0 +/- 0.93 | 0.64-1.19 |
| Leg length | 46.0 +/- 3.36 | 46.0 +/- 3.78 | 2.51-4.66 |
| Metatarsal length | 27.5 +/- 2.48 | 30.2 +/- 2.82 | 1.37-2.82 |

**Important flag (mine): the "Total" SD is not an animal-level SD.** For withers height the Total column reads 135.5 +/- 1.29, which is smaller than every centre's own SD. I recomputed it from the five centre means (135.8, 136.8, 136.8, 133.4, 134.8): their unweighted population SD is 1.294, which matches 1.29. The same check works for thorax circumference (5.53 against my 5.528) and head length (1.85 against 1.847). So the Total SD is the spread **between centre means**. For an animal-level spread, use the within-centre SDs.

**Heritability (Table 7).** Withers height h2 = 0.75 and sternal height 0.81 (high). Head length 0.36 (moderate). Arm and metacarpal length 0.18 (low). The standard errors are large (0.25-0.32, table footnote), so treat these as rough.

**Flag (mine).** Some Gudali SDs look too large for one breed: male withers height 172.66 +/- 40.92 cm and chest girth 203.78 +/- 34.44. Likewise, the Gudali head width (about 62 cm) is about the same as its head length. These look like mixed or mis-keyed data, so do not use the Gudali column for spreads. The White Fulani column looks internally plausible. Body weight CV about 26% there (mine) is high, consistent with mixed condition in field herds.

**Reading (mine).** Ear length CV about 13% and tail length CV about 10-11% are much larger than the withers height CV (about 4.5%). Horn length CV is about 25%. Small appendages vary more than the skeleton frame, as in humans (section B).

## F5. Deer: roe deer (F-S6)

**Sample.** 1,335 roe deer (Capreolus capreolus) taken by planned shooting in 1969-1979 in the Wielkopolska region of Poland: 546 from a forest area (Zielonka) and 789 from a farmland area (Czempin). Seven age classes at 2-year steps (age from tooth wear). Measured to 1 cm with a tape. Body length runs from the naso-oral plate to the last sacral vertebra. Height at the shoulder runs from the tip of the fore hoof along the leg to the shoulder. Hind foot runs from the hoof tip to the heel (Methods).

**Tables 4-6, mean +/- SD (cm), with the min-max range, adults aged 3-4 years.** Columns: Forest (n M 44, F 32), Field.

| Measure | Sex | Forest | Forest range | Field | Field range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Body length (Table 4) | M | 108.82 +/- 7.20 | 86.0-121.0 | 114.51 +/- 4.95 | 105.0-128.5 |
| Body length (Table 4) | F | 107.34 +/- 7.63 | 90.0-118.0 | 112.58 +/- 4.10 | 103.0-122.0 |
| Shoulder height (Table 5) | M | 73.16 +/- 4.68 | 63.0-87.0 | 72.67 +/- 2.74 | 65.0-80.0 |
| Shoulder height (Table 5) | F | 73.25 +/- 5.29 | 59.0-81.0 | 72.46 +/- 3.47 | 65.0-80.0 |
| Hind foot (Table 6) | M | 35.00 +/- 1.31 | 31.0-37.0 | 35.42 +/- 1.12 | 33.0-38.0 |
| Hind foot (Table 6) | F | 35.16 +/- 1.46 | 31.0-38.0 | 34.86 +/- 1.24 | 31.0-38.0 |

Yearlings (1-2 years, Table 4): forest males have a body length of 97.45 +/- 9.03 (n=100) against 108.32 +/- 5.52 in the field, so the habitat gap is largest in the young.

**Correlations (abstract, Table 7).** Body weight correlates with body length at r = 0.70, with hind foot at r = 0.51 and with shoulder height at r = 0.33 (all 1,335). The correlations are stronger in forest than field deer and in does than bucks. The Table 7 OCR is damaged beyond the "Total" row, so I take only the abstract values.

**Reading (mine).** The forest SDs are about 1.5 times the field SDs, so the habitat and population matter as much as sex. Note that roe deer show little difference between bucks and does in these frame measures.

## F6. Bird: Yellow-browed Bunting (F-S7)

**Sample.** 50 first-year birds caught on autumn migration on Daecheong Island, South Korea, and sexed by DNA: 24 males and 26 females. Measured to standard ringing protocols. Table 1, mean +/- SD (mm), with the paper's CV.

| Measure | Male | Female | CV % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wing | 78.9 +/- 1.47 | 74.9 +/- 1.21 | 3.2 |
| Tail | 64.5 +/- 1.83 | 60.6 +/- 1.87 | 4.3 |
| Tarsus | 19.3 +/- 0.54 | 19.1 +/- 0.55 | 2.9 |
| Head to bill | 30.7 +/- 0.52 | 30.7 +/- 0.54 | 1.7 |
| Bill to skull | 15.0 +/- 0.59 | 14.8 +/- 0.57 | 3.9 |
| Bill to nostril | 8.3 +/- 0.37 | 8.4 +/- 0.37 | 4.5 |

Sexual size dimorphism is largest for wing (Cohen's d = 3.00) and tail (d = 2.11). It is not significant for tarsus (p = 0.186).

**"Generic bird" (mine).** One small passerine cannot stand for all birds. What carries over is the pattern: CVs of feather lengths (wing, tail) about 3-4%, skeletal lengths (tarsus, skull) about 2-3%, and sex differences that are larger in feathers than in bones. Treat these as order-of-magnitude priors for passerines only.

- **Ear length with SD:** cattle (F-S5).

## Gaps (said plainly)

1. **Dog and cat standing joint angles:** I found no measured within-breed values with SDs in an open source. Nothing measured is in this note.
2. **Ear carriage (erect, semi-erect, drop) as an angle with a spread:** nothing measured found for any of the six species. Dog ear type is categorical in the breed standards. Using an angle would be an assumption (mine), labelled as such.
3. **Tail carriage angle:** nothing measured found.
4. **Dog limb segments, girths, ear and tail length with SDs by breed and sex:** F-S1 measured them, but its tables show only withers height. The per-breed segment values were not in the PMC copy I read.
5. **Cat outer body measures (ear, tail, head-body):** only a search summary of the Fauna of Australia (UNVERIFIED, left out). The cat figures here are bones only.
6. **Deer other than roe deer:** for white-tailed and red deer I found only ranges, not SDs. Not used.
7. **Generic bird:** one passerine species only. There are no open within-species SDs for large birds or raptors here.
8. **Age:** only the roe deer (age classes) and cattle (adults) report age at all. There are no growth-curve SDs for dogs, cats or horses here.
