"Elbow extension" is hyperextension past 0 (a positive value means the elbow goes past straight). "Knee extension" is likewise hyperextension.

## Table A5-2. Hip, knee and ankle: Roaas & Andersson (1982) mean (SD), with the AAOS (1965) and Boone & Azen (1979) values they tabulate (✔ read directly on Table 1, p. 206)

Source: Roaas A., Andersson G.B.J. (1982). Normal range of motion of the hip, knee and ankle joints in male subjects, 30–40 years of age. *Acta Orthop Scand* 53:205–208, Table 1. PDF: https://actaorthop.org/actao/article/download/28654/33535/82106 (accessed 2026-11-16); landing page https://actaorthop.org/actao/article/view/28654 (accessed 2026-11-16); DOI https://doi.org/10.3109/17453678208992202 (accessed 2026-11-16).

Population: a random sample of **men aged 30–40 from Göteborg, Sweden**: 105 subjects for the hips (210 hips), 90 for the knees, 96 for the ankles. Passive ROM, AAOS technique, one examiner, rounded to 5°. Hip extension and rotation were measured prone with the knee flexed. The AAOS and B&A columns are as printed by Roaas & Andersson. B&A is Boone & Azen (1979), *J Bone Joint Surg Am* 61(5):756–759, active ROM, 109 males aged 18 months to 54 years (Roaas's description), mean (SD).

## Sources disagree

- **Hip extension**: AAOS 28° vs Roaas 9.5° (prone, passive) vs Boone & Azen 12.1° vs CDC adults 13.5–18.1°. AAOS is far higher than any measured sample.
- **Hip rotation**: Boone & Azen about 44° each way vs Roaas about 33° vs Dempster 34–39° prone. The cause is mainly position: Boone & Azen measured sitting, Roaas prone.
- **Ankle plantar flexion**: Roaas 39.7° vs AAOS 48° vs Boone & Azen 54.3° vs CDC 49–62° (adults). That is a large spread from technique (knee flexion, passive vs active).
- **Ankle dorsiflexion**: VA 20°, AAOS 18°, Roaas 15.3°, Boone & Azen 12.2°, CDC adults 11.6–13.8°.
- **Knee flexion**: VA 140°, AAOS 134°, Roaas 143.8°, CDC 132.9–141.9°.
- **Shoulder flexion**: VA 180° vs CDC adults 164–172° vs Dempster 188° (which probably includes trunk and shoulder-girdle movement).
- **Sex**: CDC shows women with greater ROM in nearly every motion and age band (about 5–8° more plantar flexion and supination in adults). The older norms (AAOS, VA) carry no sex split.
