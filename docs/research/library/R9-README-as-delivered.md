# Round 9 reference library: Sections A to E, method only

Written 2026-11-28. This covers all of the round 9 brief (`brief.md`): Section A (the body surface), B (face, hand, nails, ear), C (generators as data, bounds, constraints), D (creatures by rule) and E (checking a generated thing by numbers). The brief has no D3.

Rules followed:
- Each item gives the citation, a free legal copy (or says there is none), and the licence.
- Content is in my own words with PDF page numbers.
- Every item has a second independent check (`.verify.md`).
- **Method only:** no code, model file, weights or data from SMPL, SCAPE, CAESAR, the Basel face data (Blanz-Vetter) or any other source were opened or used. A1, B1, E1 and E2 say what the methods still give without their data.
- Substitutes are labelled. Disagreements between sources are given both ways. Plot and figure readings and my own derivations are marked.

## Coverage map against the brief

| Brief asks | Answer, in short | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A1: how a few numbers become a whole surface | Two routes: (a) a linear map from features to PCA weights (Allen, with the cube root of weight); (b) RBF interpolation from 8 normalised measurements to PCA coefficients of a skeleton part plus a skin-displacement part (Seo). Both work on a template in correspondence | A1 §1-2 |
| A1: how many numbers explain how much variation | SMPL vertex PCA: about 90% by roughly 3-5 components (read off plot); mm errors keep falling to hundreds of components. Seo used 30 components; Pishchulin about 20 for good fits. Allen and SCAPE give no % | A1 §4-5 |
| A1: what the first components are | SCAPE (stated): weight and height; gender; abdominal fat and chest muscle; chest against hip bulk. SMPL and Pishchulin (read off figures): size/stature, then slim against heavy | A1 §3-5 |
| A1: male and female | Allen and SMPL use separate models; SCAPE uses one model with gender as a component | A1, disagreements table |
| A1: stated errors against measured bodies | Seo: girths reproduced to about 0.6 cm on average (leave-one-out, input girths only). SMPL: about 3-7 mm vertex error (plots). Pishchulin: about 4-5 mm at 30 components (plot) | A1 §2, §4, §5 |
| A1: which numbers, in what order | Stature/size, then weight or girth, then sex, then trunk proportions (mine, synthesised) | A1 end |
| A1: SCAPE/SMPL method only | Separate pose (rotations plus corrective offsets) from identity (PCA); predict joints from the surface; pose-normalise before PCA | A1 §3-4, "without the data" |
| A2: muscle belly and tendon shapes | Ellipsoid (fusiform), capped cylinder (parallel), truncated cone (triangular), multi-belly, swept patch tube; tendons as generalised cylinders (Scheepers). Deformed cylinder with 8 elliptical slices (Wilhelms) | A2 §1 |
| A2: cross-section against joint angle | Constant volume and ratio: area inversely proportional to length (Scheepers). Width and thickness each × rest/present (Wilhelms; my algebra says this does not keep volume, flagged) | A2 §1 |
| A2: how skin is laid over | Voxelise, blur, iso-surface, anchor and spring-relax (Wilhelms). Swept elliptic patch tube pushed out by implicit primitives; fall-off radius = filler fat (Scheepers) | A2 §2 |
| A2: muscles that cannot be left out | Deltoid, pectoralis major, gluteus maximus, gastrocnemius/soleus; trapezius and latissimus need wide-attachment models | A2 §3 |
| A2: fat by site and sex as a thickness map | 15-site Lipometer values for both sexes from two open papers, and Scheepers' fat-pad table. **No open continuous thickness map found** | A2 §4 |
| A3: why LBS collapses and twists | Linear average of rotations is not a rotation: radius × cos(θ/2) at 0.5/0.5 weights; 180° twist goes to zero (candy wrapper) | A3 §1 |
| A3: cost and failures of each remedy | DQS: near LBS cost, rigid, but flips past 180° and overgrows at bends. Corrective blend shapes: need data. Implicit skinning: GPU fields; fails from deep LBS starts, extreme poses, boneless bends | A3 §2-5 |
| A3: how implicit skinning keeps volume and makes contact; field needs | Per-bone HRBF fields (smooth, closed at cuts, compact support), composed per joint (union, gradient blend, contact operators); vertices projected to their own iso-value and stopped at gradient breaks | A3 §3-4 |
| B1: a face from measurements (DeCarlo, Metaxas, Stone) | Two steps: draw measurements so they stay consistent (a proportion graph spreads mean and SD from parent to child measurement), then fit a B-spline surface to a prototype face with base constraints, one constraint per measurement and a fairness objective. Stated failures: independent draws look wrong, no test for geometric consistency, using every Farkas constraint at once conflicts, the prototype shows through, measurements say nothing about curvature, ears stay coarse. Read from the thesis; the SIGGRAPH paper itself is closed | B1 §1-2 |
| B1: the morphable model, method only | PCA of shape and texture with a Gaussian prior; segments; attributes such as sex by a direction in coefficient space; only faces inside the examples; correspondence and single-image fitting fail as stated. No Basel data used | B1 §3 |
| B1: sex and age | From an open Farkas-style data set (Kesterke et al. 2016) | B1 §4 |
| B2: a hand and its nails | Reference hand warped from a photograph (Albrecht); joints placed under skin creases (Rhee: DIP and PIP under their creases, MCP about 1/3 of the way, a WEAK page-image reading); stated failures on atypical palms. Nail plate transverse curvature by finger, sex and hand (Murdan); nail configuration (Jung, widths and lengths in table images, not extracted) | B2 |
| B3: the ear as a shape from numbers | BezierPPM: 162 parameters (shape keys, armature, global transform), accuracy and stated limits. Part sizes as shares from Kent's means (indicative only; editing errors in the source). Reed: a data-driven ear model is dominated by where the ear sits, not its shape | B3 |
| C1: grammars that make shapes | Five formalisms compared on what is rewritten, parameters, context, what happens when a rule does not fit, overlap, randomness and debugging: shape grammars (sub-shape matching, emergent sub-shapes hard to debug), L-systems (parallel rewriting; overlap not prevented), split grammars (containment prevents overlap), CGA (guards, occlusion queries, snap lines), GML (Euler operators keep topology valid but allow self-intersection; undo by macros) | C1 §1-6 |
| C2: bounds carried through a computation | Interval rules with outward rounding; division by intervals containing zero; the dependency problem (why bounds blow up); affine arithmetic keeps first-order correlation; how to bound a procedural model's output; failure list | C2 |
| C3: constraints between numbers | Count degrees of freedom first (well-, under-, over-constrained); decompose into solvable parts (a failure to decompose does not mean no solution); solver properties; root selection is the hard step; numerical and homotopy methods; the sources give no method for naming the single conflicting constraint | C3 |
| D1: what shipped | Spore: retargeting one animation to any body by movement modes, gaits and particle IK, with stated limits and costs; texturing without an artist; the skin was metaball-based (one slide line) but **the skin method itself is missing**. Sims: genotype as a directed graph with recursion and repetition | D1 |
| D2: proportion as a small set of rules | Exponents by family and order (bovids, artiodactyls, carnivorans, felid bones), where they disagree, and canid covariance (size first, then pelvis against head and neck). Cross-species canid and felid head, neck and trunk exponents and neck scaling are **missing** | D2 |
| E1: plausibility by numbers | Outline: ISO 20685-1 Table 4 tolerances (1-9 mm by measure type) and Wuhrer's clamp to l standard deviations in shape space (l = 3 for made-up requests). Symmetry: no mean left-right difference in 22 pairs across 245 people; largest spread at the shoulders and wrist; **no sourced tolerance in mm**. Volume and density: scan mass within about 1.5% (Hakamada) and 1-3 kg bias (Kudzia); Clauser segment densities 1.02-1.11 kg/L. Balance: scanned centre of gravity 54.9% of stature against 56.6% measured; quiet-standing pressure centre 31-58% of foot length from the heel (mean 44.5%) | E1 |
| E2: perceptual thresholds | Limb length change: 2.7% attended and expected, up to about 20% unattended, shrinking harder to see than growing. Face ratios: acceptable ranges per figure, stricter for realistic humans. Own-body weight on avatars: "same as me" within a few per cent of BMI; women more sensitive than men. **No free measured threshold for whole-body proportions** | E2 |
