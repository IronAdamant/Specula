# Research brief, round ten: the atlas (angles, small parts, and how measurements move together)

From Claude (the lead on Specula) to the researcher, 2026-11-28. Same terms as rounds seven to nine:
for each item the citation, a working link to a free legal copy (or say there is none), the licence,
and **the numbers asked for, with the page or table they come from**: the mean, the spread (a
standard deviation, not a confidence interval of the mean), the least and greatest or the 1st, 5th,
95th and 99th percentiles where given, **by sex and by age band**, the number of people and who they
were. A second check of each file. No data set is taken into the engine; a number is typed in by
hand from its table and held by a test. Where a listed source cannot be had, a labelled substitute
held to the same standard is welcome. Where nothing measured exists, say so plainly: the engine then
uses its own estimate and labels it as its own.

**Why now.** The owner asked: "What if for human models and real world creatures, there's
measurements on every part of the body parts? An average… Hands, waists, chests, knees, feet… we
should have measurements, like general angle of the ears. And then enforcing the boundaries?" The
engine already draws a body inside measured limits for 114 dimensions and refuses what falls outside.
An audit of the library, part by part (53 parts), found: **13 parts have every length, breadth and
girth with a spread; of 16 parts that need an angle, 10 have none; and almost nothing says how
measurements move together** outside the body's main dimensions. This round asks for exactly what is
missing. It is in order of how much a generated person needs it.

---

## A. Angles of the body at rest (the largest gap)

For each: mean, SD and range by sex (and by age where it changes), how it is measured (between
which landmarks, standing or lying, on a photograph, a radiograph or a scan), and the sample.

1. **The ear on the head:** the protrusion angle (auriculocephalic or auriculomastoid angle); the
   distance of the helix from the head at the top, the middle and the lobe; the tilt of the ear's
   long axis from vertical; the ear's height against the brow and the base of the nose; left against
   right. (Rounds two and nine found distances and shares only.)
2. **The limbs' alignment:** the elbow's carrying angle; the knee's alignment standing (the
   tibiofemoral or hip-knee-ankle angle: varus and valgus), the Q angle; tibial torsion; femoral
   anteversion as far as it shows in the stance.
3. **The foot on the ground:** the foot progression angle standing and walking; the hallux valgus
   angle in healthy feet; the angle of the line of the toes' tips; **each toe's length** as a share
   of the foot's; arch height by arch type, loaded against unloaded.
4. **The trunk and neck from the side and front:** shoulder slope (the acromion below the neck's
   base, as an angle); neck inclination and the head-forward (craniovertebral) angle; the pelvis's
   tilt standing by sex; the spine's curves (cervical lordosis, thoracic kyphosis, lumbar lordosis)
   **by age from childhood to old age** (round seven has adults under 40 only); the sternal angle and
   the ribs' slope if measured on living people.
5. **The hand at rest:** the thumb's angles (abduction and rotation from the palm), the fingers'
   resting flexion at each joint, the spread between fingers; the hand's thickness at the knuckles
   and the palm.
6. **The face in profile and from the front:** the gonial (jaw) angle; the nasofrontal, nasolabial,
   mentolabial and facial convexity angles **for both sexes**; forehead slope; chin height and
   projection; brow height above the eye; the eye's slant (the palpebral fissure's inclination) in
   degrees; the nose's tip rotation and the nostril axis.

## B. Small parts with no measurements at all

1. **Eye:** visible iris diameter; the palpebral fissure's height and length by sex and age (some is
   in round two's B9: add only what is missing); the eyeball's position in the orbit (exophthalmometry
   norms).
2. **Nail:** the plate's width and length in millimetres by finger, sex and hand; its longitudinal
   curvature; the free edge's usual length (round nine has the transverse curvature only).
3. **Breast and chest wall:** the standard clinical measures (sternal notch to nipple, nipple to
   inframammary fold, nipple to nipple, base width, projection) by sex, age and size band; nipple
   position on the male chest.
4. **Knee:** breadth and girth at the kneecap, the kneecap's own size and position, by sex.
5. **Muscle as thickness or cross-section** for the parts a silhouette depends on and round five's
   A14 does not hold: deltoid, pectoralis major, trapezius, latissimus, gluteus maximus, rectus
   abdominis: by sex and training status, by ultrasound or MRI.
6. **Fat as a map:** round nine found values at 15 sites and no continuous map. Any MRI or scan study
   giving subcutaneous thickness over the trunk and limbs by sex and BMI band, even as a figure to
   read values from (say that they were read from a figure).

## C. Spreads and limits where the library has only a mean

1. **Joint ranges of motion** with standard deviations by sex and age (the CDC reference values are
   published with confidence intervals of the mean; the underlying SDs are in Soucie et al. 2011:
   any legal copy, or another normative study that gives SDs).
2. **Clavicle and scapula at rest:** more than twelve people; SDs.
3. **Segment masses and centres of mass for women** from more than fifteen people (Zatsiorsky's
   tables as adjusted by de Leva are means: is there a source with spreads, or a later scan-based
   study with them).
4. **Joint centres from surface landmarks** for women and for more than eight people.
5. **Skinfolds** with SDs by sex and age, at the sites the engine can use (triceps, subscapular,
   suprailiac, abdomen, thigh, calf).
6. **Hard limits:** for the main dimensions (stature, mass, limb lengths, head size), the least and
   greatest recorded in healthy adults, so that "outside measured people" has a number.

## D. How measurements move together (so that a drawn set is consistent)

A face or a hand drawn one number at a time looks wrong (round nine, B1). Wanted: **correlation or
covariance matrices, or regressions, with their sample**, for:

1. **The face:** between the main heights and breadths (face height, bizygomatic and bigonial
   breadth, nose length, breadth and protrusion, mouth breadth, eye spacing). Farkas's tables give
   means and variances only; is there any open data set or paper with the correlations (the 3D
   Facial Norms data's own papers, FaceBase, any anthropometric survey's head and face block: for
   instance ANSUR II's head and face dimensions, which are open: please say which face dimensions
   it has).
2. **The ear:** length, breadth, protrusion and angle together.
3. **The hand:** the 86 dimensions of round five's A9 among themselves (the survey's report may give
   a correlation table or a principal-component analysis: say what it gives).
4. **Angles with lengths:** does any angle of section A depend on stature, sex, age or a breadth
   (the carrying angle on hip breadth; the Q angle on pelvis breadth; the spine's curves on age)?
5. **The spine, the pelvis and the stance** together.

## E. Children and older people

Body dimensions with spreads for **children (2 to 17) and adults over 60** beyond stature: limb
lengths, girths, head size, hand and foot. (An open survey for each would do: for children, for
example, the anthropometric surveys behind product-safety standards; for older adults any national
survey's tables.)

## F. Animals: the same question, briefly

For the kinds the engine will make first (**dog, cat, horse, cattle, deer, a generic bird**): for
each, which body-part measurements exist **with a spread within the species** (not only a figure for
the species): limb segment lengths, girths, head length and breadth, ear length and carriage angle,
tail length, and the standing joint angles (the angles of the shoulder, elbow, stifle and hock in a
standing animal, which breed standards and veterinary texts give). Round seven has species figures
and proportions; this asks for spreads and angles.

---

## How the answers will be used

Each number becomes a row in the engine's tables with its source, its spread and its limits; a
generated body is a draw inside them and is refused outside them, with the number named. Where two
sources disagree, give both. Where only a figure can be read, say that it was read from a figure and
how exactly.
